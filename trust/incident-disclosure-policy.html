Incident Disclosure Policy

Last reviewed: July 23, 2026

We haven't had a security or data incident affecting a customer — we have no customer data to lose in the first place today (see our Privacy Policy), and no such incident has occurred. This page describes what we commit to if that ever changes, not a track record we're claiming.

What counts as an incident

  • Unauthorized access to any system that processes real business or customer-adjacent data.
  • A confirmed security vulnerability that was actually exploited, not just discovered.
  • A service outage that meaningfully affects your ability to access a product you purchased.

What we already have in place to detect one

Our systems run automated health monitoring — checking memory, storage, network connectivity to the services we depend on, and the integrity of our own data — and a crash in any core service is logged and can trigger an automatic restart and an alert to us directly. This isn't hypothetical infrastructure; it's real and running.

What we commit to if a real incident happens

  • Timely notice. If an incident affects you directly, we'll contact you at the email associated with your purchase within a reasonable time of confirming it.
  • Plain language. We'll tell you what happened, what data or access was involved, and what we're doing about it — not a legal-sounding non-answer.
  • What we're doing about it. Real remediation steps, not just an apology.

Contact

To report a suspected incident, email galaxyaek7@gmail.com directly — we read every message ourselves.
