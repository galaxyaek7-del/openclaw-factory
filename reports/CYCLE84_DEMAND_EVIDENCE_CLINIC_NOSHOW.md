# Demand evidence — Clinic No-Show product (the "$49" designated item)

**Captured:** 2026-10-14 · **Method:** live web search · **Cost:** $0
**Product under test:** `eiztlg` — "Clinic No-Show Reduction Kit", **live $59**
**Designation source:** `10_HOUR_COMMERCIAL_SPRINT_REPORT.md` H4, recorded at **$49**

## What the market actually contains

The problem is real and the segment spends money on it. Every competitor found
sells **software on a monthly subscription**, not a one-time document.

| Competitor | Price | Model |
|---|---|---|
| RecallFlow | $199/mo | SMS+email reminders, PMS sync |
| DentRecall | $119 / $325 / $599 mo | Canadian clinics, 5-touch recall |
| DentFlow | €49/mo | EU/GDPR, all-in-one CRM |
| ShowUp | $49/mo early, $99/mo std | 3-message sequence, 30% claim |
| DentReminder | ₹999 / ₹1,999 / ₹3,499 mo | WhatsApp-first, India |

## The two findings that matter

**1. A direct one-time-price competitor exists, at a LOWER price.**

> **Prophy Solutions — "Recall & Reactivation System" — $29.00**
> "Digital Download (PDF) · Instant Access After Purchase"
> Includes: recall segmentation framework, phone scripts, text templates,
> email templates, 3-touch reactivation sequence, tracking dashboard,
> objection handling guide, quick-start checklist, final action plan.

This is the same product category, the same buyer (dental hygienists / front
office), the same delivery format (a PDF), at **$29 against our $59**.

**2. Free substitutes are actively marketed.**

> **frontdesk.care** — "Templates & Scripts · Ready-to-use call scripts,
> reminder templates and front-office checklists — written for healthcare
> practices. **12 free downloadable resources.**"
> Plus a published method (reminder sequence, fair no-show policy, refundable
> deposits for $500+ procedures) showing an 18% → 8-10% no-show reduction.

## Conclusion

**This product is not viable at $59.** It is the most expensive option in a
category where a $29 direct equivalent and a free 12-resource library both
exist. The subscription competitors are not the competition — they solve the
problem with software, not with a document, so they are a different product.

The price was **not changed** — that is a founder decision, and the directive
forbids it here.

**Honest counter-argument, recorded so this is not one-sided:** the free and
$29 options are *generic hygiene* material. An argument exists for a tighter,
dental-specific, ready-to-implement system. But that argument has to be
earned with a different product, not with the same one at a higher price.
