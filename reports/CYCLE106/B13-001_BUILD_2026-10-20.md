## Why this product

Discovery is recorded in `reports/CYCLE106/DISCOVERY_2026-10-20.json` (9 `OBSERVED` sources, 5 scored
candidates, clones rejected). The chosen gap was not "another renovation budget template" — that market
is already crowded at $5–$7 with thousands of reviews. It was the specific failure those templates do
not model: money that has moved without being paid (signed quotes), and the two quiet mechanisms that
move a budget outside any change order (allowance reconciliation and forgotten credits).

Three `OBSERVED` findings drove the design, not a hunch:

* 37% of renovating homeowners went over budget and 35% upgraded materials (Houzz 2026 study).
* Allowance overages routinely run $8k–$15k, and credits owed back are the most commonly forgotten
  line item.
* Professional job costing tracks four numbers per cost line — budget, committed, actual, estimate at
  completion — and the fourth is the only one that looks forward.

## The worked example is the teaching device

Nine kitchen lines, $45,000 original budget, and five small failures instead of one dramatic one:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Approved change orders (CO-01 +1,700, CO-02 +600) | $2,300 |
| Revised budget | $47,300 |
| Committed total (84.1% of revised) | $39,800 |
| Paid to date | $16,300 |
| Unpaid committed | $23,500 |
| Forecast to complete (K-04 550, K-06 3,750, K-08 5,600) | $9,900 |
| Projected final | $49,700 |
| Variance | **−$2,400** |
| Reserve: set 4,500, drawn 2,100, remaining | **$2,400** |
| Lines over budget | 5 |
| Unsigned change order (CO-03, $450) | 1 |
| Allowance overage with no change order (A-03, $400) | 1 |
| Credit still owed (A-02, $1,200 → $400, $300 received) | $500 |

The overrun equals the remaining reserve on purpose: the reserve is already spoken for by work not yet
done. That is the sentence the workbook makes unavoidable.

## Defects found in this cycle's own first draft

Recorded in `reports/B13-001/SELF_FOUND_DEFECTS_2026-10-20.json`; all found before release.

1. **Variance was a self-reference** (`=B7-B12`), so Excel showed a blank rather than an error. Found
   by the probe, not by reading the formula.
2. **Change-order total read one row** (`='Change Orders'!$G$2+0` → $1,700 of $2,300). Found because
   the probe disagreed with the independent addition.
3. **Mislabelled percentage** — paid ÷ committed labelled as % committed. Now B7 ÷ B6 = 84.1%.
4. **A control that could not fail** — the allowance check was a tautology, then accepted any approved
   change order on the same line. Now proven by the exact change-order id (`COUNTIFS` on that id +
   `Yes`), with three explicit outcomes, and the example deliberately contains A-03 to display the
   failing path. → incident `INC-b13-001-tautological-allowance-check`.
5. **Unbalanced parentheses** in the allowance status formula made Excel refuse to open the entire
   file (`0x800A03EC`). Found by bisecting sheet by sheet; a parenthesis-balance test now guards it.

The example data also had to be corrected: forecast-to-complete had been entered as the full expected
cost for three lines, double-counting money already committed. That is the exact mistake the guide
warns about in section five, which is why it was worth fixing rather than documenting.

## Disclosed conflict: price versus the page-based policy

`economics.market_realism_check(19, 7, cfg, "gumroad_digital")` returns `(False, 7.0)` while
`gumroad_premium` returns `(True, 19.0)`. The policy measures PDF pages and cannot see a 9-sheet
workbook, so it believes a 7-page product should cost $5–$7. The product is listed at **$19.00**,
matching the sourced comparables (Etsy $6.50 and $5.38 for page-only templates, a $27 direct-response
system, $11.99 for a landlord kit) and admission that a workbook is a different artifact from a guide.

This is the fifth product in the same conflict (B12-006 $7/$29, B12-007 $5/$19, B12-008 $6/$29,
B12-009 $7/$19, B13-001 $7/$19). No price was changed silently and no threshold was weakened; the
policy is recorded as a weak signal and remains a founder decision.
