# Cycle 106 — B12-007, B12-008, B12-009 repairs (2026-10-20)

**Products:** B12-007 Digital Declutter Plan ($19) · B12-008 Moving House 8-Week Planner ($29) ·
B12-009 Pet Sitter Handover Kit ($19)
**Repair trigger:** Cycle 104 recorded `content_quality FAIL 60` for all three — 60 is the score of a
product that fails **only** the depth rule (weights: open-cleanly 30, depth 40, placeholders 10,
mojibake 10, instructions 10).
**Outcome:** `content_quality` **PASS 100** on all three. Publication not attempted.

## What was actually wrong

Thin content was the trigger, not the defect. Reading the shipped artifacts found things a word count
cannot see, including four defects in text this cycle had authored itself.

| Product | Defect | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| B12-007 | 2-page, 273-word guide | thin |
| B12-008 | 2-page, 273-word guide | thin |
| B12-008 | workbook: 4 sheets, **1** formula — no calculation to speak of | thin |
| B12-008 | Summary row `Deposits at risk (USD)` was `SUM` of **every** deposit | label overstates the formula |
| B12-008 | guide: worked example called a 1,950 quote the "maximum score" and said the cheapest quote takes the same price score | text contradicts the workbook |
| B12-008 | guide: "a day after the old close" beside example dates two days apart | text contradicts itself |
| B12-008 | Summary formatted counting rows as money | format |
| B12-008 | guide ×2: "the Dashboard" — this workbook has no Dashboard sheet | cross-reference to nothing |
| B12-009 | 2-page, 233-word guide; "a eye that cannot open"; "The form in section six" (it is eleven); "Pages two and three are the part that matters" | thin + typo + wrong cross-reference |

The "Dashboard" mistake is the traceable one: B12-007's workbook really does have a `Dashboard` sheet,
B12-008's guide was written alongside it, and nothing checks a guide against its own workbook's sheet
names. That single missing control produced both the cross-reference and the arithmetic error.

## What was rebuilt

| Artifact | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| B12-007 guide PDF | 4,036 B · 2 pp · 273 words | **14,824 B · 5 pp · 2,817 words** · 11 sections |
| B12-007 workbook | untouched | 9,374 B · 6 sheets · 10 formulas (holds the Cycle-104 `Dashboard` file-level repair) |
| B12-008 guide PDF | 4,311 B · 2 pp · 273 words | **17,339 B · 6 pp · 3,265 words** |
| B12-008 workbook | 7,629 B · 4 sheets · 1 formula | **16,860 B · 7 sheets · 129 formulas · 9 validations** |
| B12-009 guide PDF | 4,283 B · 2 pp · 233 words | **16,718 B · 7 pp · 2,729 words** · 13 sections + 5 forms |

B12-008's at-risk row is now honest arithmetic, not a sum:

- `Total deposits held (USD)` = `SUM(Deposits!B2:B6)`
- `Deposits with photos done (USD)` = `SUMIF`
- `Deposits still at risk (USD)` = `=$B$18-$B$19`

Number formats are per row (percent, integer counts, money) instead of one money format for the whole
block. The guide's worked example was corrected to the arithmetic the workbook actually performs
(`40 × (1 - 250 / 1,700) = 34`, totals 70 / 94 / 91, the 1,950 mover shortlisted) and then **proved
against Excel** by entering it into a throwaway copy and recalculating.

## Price — disclosed, not changed

No price was changed. `economics.market_realism_check` reads as follows (the $1-per-page ceiling binds
on PDF pages, which is why these short form-led products read low on the general tier):

| Product | Manifest | `gumroad_digital` | `gumroad_elite` |
|---|---|---|---|
| B12-007 | $19 | rejected → **$5.00** | **accepted at $19** |
| B12-008 | $29 | rejected → **$6.00** | **accepted at $29** |
| B12-009 | $19 | rejected → **$7.00** (accepted at $7) | **accepted at $19** |

The auto-distribution path uses the general tier, so left alone it would silently re-price these
products down. The policy counts PDF pages only; the workbooks — the part a buyer actually uses and
where the depth now lives — are invisible to it. Same shape already recorded for B12-006 and B12-010.
This is a founder decision, so it is disclosed with both readings and left open.

## Self-caught defects (disclosed)

Four of the B12-008 defects and both B12-009 prose defects were in content **this cycle authored**,
found by reading the extracted text of the shipped files before release rather than by any gate. They
were caught, fixed, and are recorded as incidents (`INC-b12-008-guide-contradicts-workbook`,
`INC-b12-008-mislabeled-summary-calculation`) with the control that would have caught each earlier. A
separate low-severity incident records the probe undercount (`INC-excel-probe-formula-undercount`).
No defect reached a customer; none of these products has ever been published.

One reporting slip of this cycle is also recorded honestly: an early status summary quoted a **stale**
sweep file (a scratch script held a hard-coded earlier path), which understated the workbook formula
count. It was corrected by re-reading the fresh sweep; the numbers in this report come from the emitted
evidence, not from memory.

## Also observed (not fixed here)

`data/product_batch_12.json` entries for B12-006..009 carry `audience` and `problem` but no `outcome`,
so the QA sweep's core-value layer scores 75 instead of 100 for those four products. The registry is
generated by `scripts/build_batch12_pages_qa.py`, which must not be run, so the field was **not**
hand-edited. Its stale `READY_FOR_REVIEW` state is already a recorded incident (`INC-registry-drift`).

Evidence files are named by the **UTC** date. A run that straddles the UTC date rollover produces a
second file rather than replacing the first; the integrity gate reads the newest, which is correct,
but the stale sibling was removed this cycle so nothing ambiguous is left on disk.
