# systeme.io free plan: what it covers, and the three limits that force an upgrade

*Disclosure: I'm an affiliate for systeme.io. If you click the link in this article and later pay for a plan, I earn a commission at no extra cost to you. This article is written so that it is useful even if you never click anything — including the cases where I tell you not to upgrade.*

Most "is the free plan enough?" articles are written to get you to upgrade. The free plan has a real ceiling, and it's easy to hit without noticing. This is what the plan actually includes, where the walls are, and a rule for deciding whether you're behind a wall or not.

## What the free plan actually includes

From systeme.io's own pricing page (last updated April 2026):

| Feature | Free plan |
|---|---|
| Contacts | 2,000 |
| Sales funnels | 3 |
| Courses | 1 |
| Students | 500 |
| Blog | 1 |
| Custom domain | 1 |
| Affiliate program (your own) | 1 |
| Email campaigns | 1 |
| Automation rules | 1 |
| Emails sent | unlimited |
| Transaction fees | 0% |
| Credit card required | no |
| Expires | never |

Two things in that table are worth pausing on, because they're unusual:

**Unlimited emails on the free tier.** Most platforms meter sends, not contacts. Here the meter is contact count, so the cost of writing to your list every week is zero right up to 2,000 people.

**No credit card to start.** You can build a funnel, connect Stripe or PayPal, and take a real payment without entering payment details for the platform itself. That matters if you want to validate an offer before you take on any recurring cost at all.

## The three limits that actually force an upgrade

Of everything in that table, only three limits tend to block a working business. The others (one course, one blog, one custom domain) are limits on *breadth*, and most solo operators don't need breadth before they need revenue.

### 1. 2,000 contacts

This is the real ceiling. If you're collecting leads into a funnel, 2,000 contacts is a meaningful list — for many solo businesses it takes a year or more to reach. The next tier, Startup at $17/month, raises it to 5,000.

The honest test: **are you actually near 2,000, or does it just sound close?** Check the number in your dashboard. If you're at 400, this limit is not your problem, and paying $17/month to remove it buys you nothing.

### 2. 3 sales funnels

Three funnels sounds generous until you count how people actually build. Most operators run one funnel per offer, plus a lead magnet funnel, plus a version of each for a different traffic source. That's four.

But the same test applies: if you have one working funnel, you have room for two more experiments. The 3-funnel limit only bites after you have something that works and want to branch it.

### 3. 1 automation rule and 1 email campaign

This is the limit people underestimate, and it's the one I'd point at first.

An automation rule is a trigger-and-action pair ("when someone tags themselves X, send email Y"). One rule runs one thing. If you want a welcome sequence, a tag-based branch, and a re-engagement trigger, that's three cards on the automation board — and you have one.

An email campaign is a one-off send to a segment. One campaign at a time is enough for a monthly newsletter and not enough for anything resembling lifecycle email.

If your plan requires more than one automated sequence, the free plan will not hold it, and no amount of clever funnel building gets around it.

## The decision rule

Don't upgrade on contact count alone. Upgrade when **one specific thing you need is blocked**, and name it before you pay:

- *"I need a fourth funnel for a second offer."* → upgrade.
- *"I need a second automated sequence."* → upgrade.
- *"I have 2,000 contacts and my list is still growing."* → upgrade.
- *"I want to look more professional."* → not a reason. Nobody has ever seen your plan tier.

If none of those sentences is true today, the free plan is the correct plan, and $17/month is $204/year spent to feel productive.

Worth knowing when you do upgrade: Startup is $17/month for 5,000 contacts, 10 funnels and 5 courses; Webinar is $47/month for 10,000 contacts, 50 funnels, 20 courses and evergreen webinars; Unlimited is $97/month with no listed limits. All tiers keep unlimited email sends and 0% transaction fees. Annual billing gives two months free, which is the only discount I can verify from the page — I haven't tested whether the annual plans suit a business that might outgrow a tier mid-year.

## What I did not verify

Being specific about the limits of my own research, since the whole point of this article is that limits should be named:

- **Refund and cancellation behaviour of commissions and plans.** The pricing page says you can cancel any time and revert to the free plan at the next billing date. It does not describe pro-rata refunds.
- **Whether these limits changed since April 2026.** That is the date on the page I read on 20 October 2026. If you are reading this much later, check the page.
- **Whether the free plan is enough for a *team*.** Everything above assumes one operator. Multi-user setups have their own limits I did not test.
- **Migration.** systeme.io advertises free migration, including from other platforms. I have not done one, so I have no opinion on how it goes.

The short version: the free plan is not a trial, it doesn't expire, and it is genuinely enough below 2,000 contacts with one automated sequence. Pay when a named limit blocks a named task — not before.
