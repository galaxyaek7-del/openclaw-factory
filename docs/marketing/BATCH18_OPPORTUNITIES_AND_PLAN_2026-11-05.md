## Drafts you can paste (edit to your voice; disclose that you sell a tool where the site asks for it)

**D1: Quora, cost per mile**
> The method is the same whether it is a car or a truck: add up every cost over a period and divide by every mile driven,
> loaded or empty. Split it in two: fixed costs per month (payment, insurance, permits, software, your own pay) divided by
> the miles you actually drive in a month, plus variable cost per mile (fuel = price per gallon / mpg, maintenance, tires,
> tolls). Then, if some miles are empty, the rate you need per LOADED mile is the total cost per mile divided by (1 - empty
> share). A load that pays well per loaded mile can still lose money once the empty move to reach it is counted. I wrote the
> formula up with a worked example here: [free article link]. (Disclosure: I also sell a spreadsheet that does this per load;
> the article is complete without it.)

**D2: BiggerPockets, buying a small business**
> Most small deals are priced as a multiple of seller's discretionary earnings: net income plus the owner's pay, interest,
> depreciation, one-time costs and personal perks. The multiple matters more than the arithmetic and varies by industry and
> size, so get comparable recent sales, and tie SDE to three years of tax returns. Equipment and real estate are usually
> priced separately. Then test whether the price can carry its debt: cash after paying a replacement manager divided by annual
> debt service; lenders often look for about 1.25. Worked example and the steps: [free article link]. (Disclosure: I sell a
> workbook for this; the article is free.)

**D3: BiggerPockets, Section 179 and leasing**
> Compare the two after tax, over the same period, in today's money, not by monthly payment. Buying: down payment, loan
> payments split into interest and principal, depreciation (MACRS or an expensing election, which your accountant decides),
> the tax saved by interest and depreciation, and what you net at resale after tax on the gain and the loan balance. Leasing:
> the payments, generally deductible if it is a true operating lease. Section 179 cannot create a loss, so check you will have
> the income to use it. Steps with an example: [free article link].

**D4: Quora, wholesale vs retail pricing**
> "50% margin" usually means the retailer keeps half of the shelf price (keystone), which is a 100% markup on their cost.
> Work back from your landed cost: wholesale = cost / (1 - your target margin); if a distributor sits in between, the
> retailer pays wholesale / (1 - distributor margin); required shelf price = that / (1 - retailer margin). Categories differ
> (grocery usually lower), so confirm in writing. Formulas and an example: [free article link].

**D5: Indie Hackers, "Spent $1k on ads. Zero sales"**
> One number would have told you before spending: break-even buyers = (ad budget + fixed launch costs) / (price x (1 - refund
> rate) x (1 - fee rate) - support cost per buyer). Compare it with your list size: if you would need more than a few percent of
> an active list to buy, the launch depends on paid traffic, and ads without a list or a tested page are the riskiest way to
> learn conversion. Run a pessimistic case and decide a stop rule before you spend. Worked example: [free article link].
