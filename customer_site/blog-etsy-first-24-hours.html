  1. Save the notice exactly as received. Screenshot the email and any Shop Manager banner. The notice states the suspension type (temporary vs permanent) and usually the reason — everything downstream depends on reading it precisely.
  2. Confirm you can still sign in. A suspended account can usually still log in (needed for the Appeals Center later). If login itself fails, that is a different problem — resolve access before anything else.
  3. Read the cited policy, not summaries of it. Open Etsy's actual Seller Policy section named in the notice. Paraphrases drift; the exact rule text is what reviewers enforce.
  4. Freeze the shop state. Do not edit listings, open new cases, or message support yet. Every change before documentation muddies what reviewers see. Screenshot current listings, balances, and open orders first.
  5. Preserve evidence now. Download order records, message threads, supplier invoices, licenses, and creation-process proof (photos, design files). Evidence created later looks weaker than evidence captured today.
  6. Write down the timeline. Dates of the notice, the triggering events you can identify, and anything you already fixed. Appeals live or die on dated specifics.
  7. Do nothing public for 24 hours. No second account (it becomes an evasion pattern), no angry support ticket, no social media thread. One calm, documented appeal beats five frantic ones — and the appeal window (6 months for permanent suspensions) gives you time.
