Rent vs Buy Home Calculator: Net Worth Year by Year

Compare buying a home with renting and investing the difference: net worth under each choice for every year up to 30, the break-even year, and the answer for the number of years you plan to stay.

Comparing a mortgage payment with a rent cheque leaves most of the decision out. Buying ties up a down payment and closing costs, adds property tax, insurance and upkeep, and pays you back through equity and price growth, minus selling costs. Renting keeps that cash free to invest.

This calculator puts both choices on the same footing. For every year up to 30 it computes the home's value, the remaining loan, the full cost of owning, the rent, and a renter's investment account that starts with the down payment and closing costs and receives the difference in yearly costs. It compares the net worth of each choice if you sold or stopped at the end of that year.

Summary answers the question for the number of years you plan to stay, shows the break-even year, and states which choice is ahead.

What is included

  • Net worth of buying vs renting-and-investing for every year up to 30
  • Full cost of owning: mortgage, property tax, insurance, upkeep, HOA, closing and selling costs
  • Renter's investment account built from the down payment and the cost difference
  • Answer for the number of years you plan to stay, plus the break-even year
  • Loan balance computed with the standard amortisation formula
  • PDF guide with the formulas, a worked example and the limits stated plainly

Who it is for

  • First-time buyers deciding whether to buy now or keep renting
  • Renters weighing a move to a more expensive area
  • Couples planning how long they will stay in a city
  • Financial coaches who want a transparent rent-vs-buy model

How it works

1) On Settings, enter the home price, down payment, mortgage rate and term, closing costs, property tax, insurance, upkeep, HOA fees, expected price growth and selling costs. 2) Enter the rent you would pay, its yearly increase, renter's insurance and the return you expect on invested savings. 3) Enter the number of years you plan to stay. 4) Read Summary; Years shows every year.

Limits

  • Taxes are not modelled: no mortgage-interest deduction, no capital-gains tax on investments or on the home sale.
  • Fixed-rate mortgage only; PMI, refinancing and adjustable rates are not included.
  • When renting costs more than owning, the renter draws the difference from investments, which can go below zero.
