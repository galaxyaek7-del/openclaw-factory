Client wants free extra work? Here is the sequence that protects your rate

The request is rarely hostile. It is small, it is friendly, and if you absorb it this once you have taught the client that asking is free. What protects you is not saying no — it is having a written path ready before you need one.

1. Do not answer in the moment with a yes or a no

Reply with a boundary and a route. “That request sits outside the scope we agreed, so I will price it separately and we can decide where it fits.” That is not adversarial. It is what a professional process sounds like, and it takes one sentence.

2. Give them a cheaper option than the one they are asking for

Price the add-on — but also offer a trade: “I can add this, or swap it against something currently included; your call, budget stays the same.” Most clients choose the swap. The negotiation stops being about whether you will work, and becomes about what they want instead.

3. Cap revisions before they start, not after

Two rounds included. Define what a revision actually is — a minor edit, not a redesign — and price anything beyond per round. The expensive request is never “add a page”; it is the fourth complete rewrite nobody scoped.

4. Make the IP clause do work for you

Ownership transfers on full payment. This is not a trick. It is the only thing that makes a change order enforceable, and it means an unpaid extra deliverable is never simply handed over and forgotten.

5. Fix the late fee before it is late

State a specific flat fee or monthly rate in the contract, and check what your own jurisdiction allows first. A late fee agreed at signing is a conversation. A late fee introduced after the invoice is an argument.

6. Read the change-order clause you already signed

Most freelancers already have a clause requiring written approval and price before extra work begins. If it is there, a scope change is not a conflict with your client — it is a process you are both following. If it is not there, you can still send a retroactive change order now. It is harder, but far better than absorbing the hours.

7. If you sign nothing at all, do this

Put the boundary in the very first reply, every time, in writing: “Anything not listed above is a separate quote.” It costs one line and it removes most of the ambiguity that scope creep feeds on.
