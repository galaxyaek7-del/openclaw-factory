Turo invoice dispute: the 48-hour window and what to file

1. The clock is the whole problem

This is the part most people miss, and it is the reason a dispute that should have been obvious is lost.

  1. You have 48 hours from the moment you receive the host's invoice to file a dispute. That is the window.
  2. After 48 hours, it is gone. Turo's own wording: once the dispute window closes, customer support agents cannot reopen it. There is no appeal of a missed window.
  3. You can still change your mind inside the 48 hours and just pay it — but that ends the dispute too.
  4. The host then has 72 hours to respond. They can cancel the invoice, send a revised one, or escalate to support.

Source: help.turo.com, "How to dispute an invoice from your host" (guests).

2. What Turo actually decides on

Not on who is more persuasive. Turo states it plainly: "We rely on photo evidence in deciding the outcome of a dispute." So the dispute is a documentation problem, not an argument.

3. Common charges, and what to check on each

  • Fuel — check the mileage figure against your own trip record. Hosts use a standard distance; a large overage on a short trip is worth querying.
  • Mileage — ask which route they used. A detour that was never agreed is a different charge.
  • Tolls — these should be traceable to a receipt or a transponder log.
  • Cleaning — Turo's cleaning rules exist to stop ordinary wear being billed as damage. Ordinary dust and normal interior wear are not a cleaning charge.
  • Damage — needs photos and a repair estimate. Wear and tear is excluded from damage claims by definition.

4. What to write in the dispute box

The box is short. Being specific beats being angry, and it beats being long.

  1. Name the specific charge you are disputing and the amount.
  2. State the reason in one sentence, factually.
  3. Attach the photos that prove it — with their metadata intact.
  4. Attach any document that ties the charge to a real cost.

Then stop replying in the dispute thread and use the ticket. Turo escalates disputes to support as a ticket, and the ticket is where the decision is made.

5. Two things that will make it worse

  • Never pay a host directly for a dispute. Turo's wording: paying the host directly violates the Gray Market transaction policy and can limit Turo's ability to help you — including if the host re-invoices you for something you already paid.
  • Don't argue about goodwill after the window closes. Once 48 hours pass, support cannot reopen it. Put the effort into the first 48 hours instead of the message you send afterwards.

6. If it goes further

Escalated claims are handled by Turo, not by you. Turo then sends the claim to a third-party appraiser, usually photo-based, with a field inspection if photo quality is low. After that the host's insurance or your card is charged for any balance, or the account goes to collections.

Which is the real reason the 48-hour window matters: after the window, you have no cheap path left.
