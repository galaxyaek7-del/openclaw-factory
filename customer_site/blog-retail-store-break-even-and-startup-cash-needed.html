Retail Store Break-Even: How Much Cash You Need Before the Shop Turns Profitable

Most new shops fail from running out of cash during the ramp, not from a bad idea.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $39 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

Before you sign a lease, answer three questions: how much will opening really cost, what monthly sales cover the fixed costs, and how deep does the cash go before the shop pays you back? All figures here are illustrative; your numbers will differ.

The formulas

  • Startup spend = build-out + opening inventory + deposits + licences + pre-opening costs, plus a contingency. Build-outs rarely come in under budget.
  • Contribution margin = gross margin % - variable cost % (card fees, sales labour).
  • Break-even monthly sales = fixed monthly costs / contribution margin. Include your own draw in the fixed costs.
  • Cumulative cash = -startup spend + the operating profit of every month so far.

Worked example

Startup costs of $129,500 plus a 10% contingency make a $142,450 startup spend. Fixed costs are $20,430 a month. With a 48% gross margin and 8.6% variable costs, 39.4% of each sales dollar is left to cover them, so break-even sales are $51,853 a month, 61% of the $85,000 the shop is expected to reach.

Sales start at 35% of that level and grow over 12 months. The shop first turns profitable in month 6, the cash position bottoms out at a deficit of $166,203 and the investment is paid back in month 21.

What to test

  • A ramp twice as slow as you hope. It deepens the cash hole and pushes payback out.
  • A higher rent. Every extra $1,000 of fixed cost raises break-even sales by about $2,538.
  • Opening smaller. A smaller first version often has a shorter payback.
