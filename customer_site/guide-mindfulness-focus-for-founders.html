The 5-Minute Reset for Founders Who Juggle Too Much

For freelancers, founders, and operators who switch contexts 20+ times a day — a short, no-app routine you can try between meetings.
Why context-switching is expensive

When you jump from a client call to a budget sheet to Slack, your brain keeps a “tab” open on each. Research on attention residue shows the prior task lingers for minutes. A brief, structured pause — naming what you just finished, what matters next, and one small action — helps close those tabs.

A useful 5-minute routine (try it once today)

  1. 60s — Close the last tab (30–60s): Write one sentence: “I just finished ___; next, ___ matters most.” No judgment, just a handoff.
  2. 90s — One breath + scan: Three slow breaths. Notice shoulders, jaw, eyes. No diagnosis, just observation. If you feel tension, note it as “tension” — not a medical label.
  3. 90s — Choose one: Pick the single next step that moves your most important task 1% forward. Write it as “Next: ___ (≤25 min).”
  4. 30s — Close: Write when you’ll revisit this page (e.g., “after stand-up”).

Use a plain notebook or any notes app. Repeat for 3 days and notice whether the handoff feels smoother — that’s your evidence.
