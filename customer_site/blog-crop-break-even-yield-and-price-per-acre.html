Crop Break-Even Yield and Price: How to Budget Each Crop Per Acre

The basic enterprise budget, with a worked example.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $29 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

An enterprise budget lists, for one crop, the expected revenue and every cost per acre, so you can see which crops pay and what each needs to break even before any seed goes in the ground. Free university tools for this exist; the steps below are the method they all follow.

The formulas

  • Revenue per acre = expected yield x price.
  • Variable cost per acre = seed + fertilizer + chemicals + labour + fuel and repairs + other inputs.
  • Contribution margin per acre = revenue - variable cost. Fixed cost per acre = whole-farm fixed costs / total acres.
  • Net return per acre = contribution margin - fixed cost per acre.
  • Break-even yield = (variable + fixed cost per acre) / price. Break-even price = (variable + fixed cost per acre) / yield.

Worked example

Whole-farm fixed costs of $47,100 (land, tax and insurance, machinery ownership, overhead and an $18,000 owner labour draw) spread over 113 acres add $416.81 per acre to every crop. Corn at 175 bushels an acre and $4.60 earns $805 per acre against $390 of variable costs, leaving a net return of $-2 per acre. The break-even yield is 175 bushels at that price, a margin of safety of -0%.

How to use the break-even numbers

  • Compare the break-even yield with your own five-year average, not the best year you remember.
  • Compare the break-even price with forward contracts and elevator bids.
  • If every crop is profitable per acre but the farm loses money, fixed costs are too high for the acres.

Count your own labour as a cost, so the budget shows whether the farm pays you.
