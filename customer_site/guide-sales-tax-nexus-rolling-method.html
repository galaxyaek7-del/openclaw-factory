Multi-state sales tax nexus: the rolling-12-month method (2026)

For US remote sellers shipping into states they have never visited. Free method below; not tax advice.
Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $79 nexus tracking workbook implementing this method (Sales Tax Nexus Tracker). This guide is the method itself, free to use. Not tax advice — verify every threshold with the state revenue department or a CPA.

1. Why sellers get caught

Since the 2018 Wayfair decision, US states can require remote sellers to register, collect, and remit sales tax once sales cross their economic nexus threshold — with no physical presence needed. Several states changed their thresholds again in 2024–2026. Miss a crossing and the state can assess back taxes plus penalties retroactive to the date you crossed, not the date you noticed.

2. Track on a rolling 12 months, not calendar year

Several states measure the trailing 12 months, not January to December. A calendar-year tally can miss a midyear crossing. Keep one running log of sales by state and check the trailing-12-month total every month.

3. Know that rules differ per state

The common pattern is $100,000 in sales, but some states differ, some count marketplace sales toward your threshold and some do not, and registration windows are often 30–60 days. Verify the current rule with each state revenue department before acting — not from a blog post, including this one.

4. Separate what counts from what does not

Track direct-channel sales and marketplace sales in separate columns. Inventory sitting in a third-party warehouse can also create physical nexus regardless of sales volume.

5. When you cross, move in this order

  1. Confirm the threshold in writing with the state.
  2. Register for a sales tax permit inside the state window.
  3. Determine the correct local rate per shipping address.
  4. Begin collecting at checkout.
  5. Diary the filing schedule the state assigns — missing a filing deadline can trigger penalties even when the tax was collected.
