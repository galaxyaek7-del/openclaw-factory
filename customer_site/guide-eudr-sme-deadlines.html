EUDR for small businesses: which deadline applies to you (2026)

For SMEs handling coffee, cocoa, soy, palm oil, rubber, cattle products, wood — or anything derived from them. Free method below; confirm everything against the European Commission's current guidance before acting.

Regulatory information, not legal advice. EUDR has been amended and delayed before — dates below reflect the position as of late 2026 and may change.

1. Check scope before checking dates

EUDR covers seven commodity groups (cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya, wood) and listed derivatives — chocolate, furniture, tyres, paper and more. If none of your products touches these, stop here: you likely have no EUDR obligation. The most common SME mistake is preparing for a regulation that does not cover them.

2. Find your role — it decides your workload

Upstream operators placing products on the EU market carry full due diligence (geolocation, legality evidence, Due Diligence Statement). Downstream operators and traders mostly keep records: supplier info and DDS reference numbers, five years. Micro/small primary operators may use a one-off simplified declaration. Same product, very different workload — role first, always.

3. Read your deadline off role + size

Current position: 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators; 30 June 2027 for micro and small enterprises. Confirm your size classification honestly — the extra six months for small firms is real, but only if you actually qualify.

4. The four first steps that matter

  1. List every product against the commodity groups — in or out, with CN codes where possible.
  2. Assign your role per flow (operator, downstream, trader) — different flows can mean different roles.
  3. Ask suppliers now for plot geolocation and legality evidence — suppliers take months, not weeks, to respond.
  4. Register for the EU Information System (TRACES) early — filings, not understanding, are where deadlines bite.

5. Do not wait for another delay

EUDR was postponed before, and outdated advice still circulates. The Commission has stated there will be no further postponement. Supplier engagement is the long pole — starting late compresses the one step you cannot accelerate.
