Wedding Budget & Vendor Payment Planner

Split your budget across categories, record every quote and contract, track deposits and the balances still due, and see at once which category has gone over.

Wedding budgets slip one category at a time: a venue quote a little over plan, a few extra guests on the catering contract, a deposit nobody wrote down. By the time it shows, the money is committed.

This workbook keeps the budget and the contracts in one place. You set a total and split it across categories; for each category you record what you have actually agreed with the vendor, what you have paid and when the balance is due. Every category over its plan is flagged, and the Summary shows money committed, paid, still owed, and the cost per guest.

It is built for the way weddings are paid: deposits first, balances weeks before the day, so the Vendors sheet lists every balance with its due date.

What is included

  • Budget split across 15 categories with planned amounts from your total
  • Contracted amount, payments made, balance due and due date for every vendor
  • OVER PLAN flag the moment a contract exceeds its category
  • Summary: committed, paid, still owed, uncommitted budget, cost per guest
  • Planned shares check: tells you when the split does not add up to 100%
  • PDF guide with a worked example and a field-by-field reference

Who it is for

  • Couples planning their own wedding
  • Parents or family members managing the payments
  • Wedding planners and coordinators tracking several vendors for a client

How it works

1) On Budget, enter your total budget and guest count. 2) On Vendors, adjust the categories and the planned share for each (the example split is only a starting point; the shares should add up to 100%). 3) As quotes arrive, enter the vendor and the contracted amount; record each payment as you make it and the date the balance is due. 4) Any category marked OVER PLAN needs a decision: cut elsewhere or raise the total. 5) The Summary shows what is committed, paid and still owed.

Limits

  • One currency; convert foreign vendors' quotes yourself.
  • It does not send reminders; check the due dates regularly.
  • Tips, taxes and service charges must be included in the contracted amount you enter.
