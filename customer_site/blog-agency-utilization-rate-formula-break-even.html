Agency Utilization Rate: Formula, Break-Even Utilization and the Hiring Signal

The one number that decides whether a services business makes money.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $39 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

An agency sells time, so the share of available hours that are billed decides the profit. Most owners watch revenue and miss that one person is billing half their hours and costing more than they earn while another is at 92% and close to leaving.

The formulas

  • Utilization = billed hours / available hours for the same person and period. For a team, sum billed hours and sum available hours, then divide the totals. Averaging individual percentages gives the wrong answer.
  • Loaded cost = salary x (1 + overhead %). Cost per available hour = loaded cost / (hours per week x working weeks).
  • Break-even utilization = loaded cost / (bill rate x hours per week x working weeks).
  • Bench cost = hours below target x weeks x cost per available hour.

Worked example

A junior designer costs $65,000 loaded, bills $65 an hour and bills 20 of 40 hours, so utilization is 50%. To cover the cost at that rate they must bill 54.3% of available hours: below break-even. A developer billing 37 of 40 hours is at 92.5%: the best-looking number on the team and a retention risk, because nothing is left for sick days, training or sales support.

A hiring signal that is not a busy week

Compare the hours the signed pipeline needs per week with the spare hours people still have before reaching their target. Only the shortfall justifies a hire, and only if the new role's own break-even utilization is realistic at your rate.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using planned hours instead of billed hours from a time tracker.
  • Leaving benefits and overhead out of the loaded cost.
  • Counting internal and sales time as billable.
