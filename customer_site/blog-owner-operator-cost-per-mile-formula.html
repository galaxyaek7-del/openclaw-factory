Owner-Operator Cost per Mile: The Formula and What Empty Miles Do to It

Fixed cost per mile, variable cost per mile, and the rate you must earn on loaded miles.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $39 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

Most small trucking operations know their truck payment and fuel but not their cost per mile, so they accept loads that feel fine and quietly lose money on the miles driven empty. Here is the calculation.

The formula

  1. Fixed cost per mile = total fixed costs per month / planned miles per month. Fixed costs include the truck payment, insurance, permits, ELD and software, parking, accounting, and your own pay.
  2. Variable cost per mile = fuel (diesel price / miles per gallon) + maintenance + tires + tolls + other costs per mile.
  3. Total cost per mile = fixed + variable, for every mile driven, loaded or empty.
  4. Break-even rate per loaded mile = total cost per mile / (1 - empty share of miles).

Worked example

With $11,300 of fixed costs a month (including a $6,000 owner draw) over 9,500 planned miles, fixed cost is $1.189 per mile. Adding $0.831 of variable cost gives a total of $2.021 per mile driven. If 15% of miles are empty, the break-even rate is $2.377 per loaded mile.

Now a load from Denver to Omaha: $1,050 linehaul plus $205 fuel surcharge for 541 loaded miles is $2.32 per loaded mile, which looks acceptable. But the truck must drive 300 empty miles to reach it. Over 841 miles the revenue is $1.49 per mile against a cost of $2.02, so the load loses $444.

The lesson: judge every load by revenue per total mile as well as per loaded mile.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving your own pay out of fixed costs, so every load looks profitable.
  • Using last year's fuel price. Re-check when diesel moves by 25 cents.
  • Setting planned miles higher than the ELD logs show you actually drive.
