Subscription renewal compliance: the owner-rule audit (2026)

For businesses with memberships, auto-renewals, or free-to-paid flows. Free method below; not legal advice.
Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $69 compliance kit implementing this method (Subscription Renewal Compliance Kit). This guide is the method itself, free to use. Not legal advice — verify with qualified counsel.

1. Why this is enforced now

Regulators pursue subscription practices under long-standing unfair-practice and online-shopper statutes, with recent settlements reaching tens of millions and beyond. Roughly 30 US states run their own auto-renewal laws, some stricter than the federal baseline. A new federal rulemaking effort restarted in 2026. The scrutiny is current, not theoretical.

2. The three things auditors check

Disclosure (are all material terms clear before billing info is taken), consent (is there proof the customer agreed to the recurring charge), and cancellation (is quitting at least as easy as signing up, through the same medium, without forced retention calls). Fail any one and the other two do not save you.

3. Walk your own signup-to-cancel path

Enroll like a customer, screenshot every screen, then cancel like a customer and count the steps, the dark patterns, and the save-attempts. If cancellation takes meaningfully more effort than enrollment, that gap is the finding.

4. Run the 30/60/90-day remediation

  1. Days 1–30: fix disclosure placement and consent proof; log every change.
  2. Days 31–60: rebuild the cancellation path to mirror enrollment; remove forced retention steps.
  3. Days 61–90: check state-law deltas for every state you sell into; calendar annual re-audits.
