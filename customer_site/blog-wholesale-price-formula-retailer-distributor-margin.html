Wholesale Price Formula: Work Back From Cost Through the Retailer and Distributor

A product that works direct can lose money the moment a retailer stocks it.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $29 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

Retailers expect to keep roughly half of the shelf price, distributors take a share too, and what is left must still cover your cost, packaging, freight and a profit. Work the price back in this order. Channel margins differ by category and are stated on each party's own selling price; confirm yours in writing.

Margin is not markup

A 50% margin means the retailer keeps half of the shelf price. It equals a 100% markup on cost. Mixing the two is the commonest pricing error.

The formulas

  • Your wholesale price = landed unit cost / (1 - your target margin).
  • Distributor price (what the retailer pays) = wholesale price / (1 - distributor margin).
  • Required retail price = distributor price / (1 - retailer margin).
  • From a planned shelf price instead: maximum wholesale price = shelf price x (1 - retailer margin) x (1 - distributor margin).

Worked example

A candle costs $4.10 landed. At a 55% target margin, a 25% distributor margin and a 50% retailer margin the wholesale price is $9.11, the distributor price $12.15 and the required shelf price $24.30. A planned $24.00 shelf price clears that by $-0.30.

At $24.00 the most you can charge is $9.00, a gross profit of $4.90 per unit. Selling the same candle direct nets $13.96 after fees and shipping, so one direct sale equals about 2.8 wholesale units. That is a reason to set a minimum order, not to refuse wholesale.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using ex-factory cost instead of landed cost (freight, duties, packaging).
  • Forgetting promotional allowances and markdown support.
  • Setting an MSRP from your direct price without checking the channel math.
