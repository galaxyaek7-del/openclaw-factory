Online Course Launch Break-Even: How Many Buyers You Need Before You Spend

The one number to measure the launch against, calculated before you place the bet.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $29 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

A course launch is a bet: you pay for creation, tools and ads, then hope enough people buy. The bet is easier to judge before you place it. The conversion rates below are illustrative, not benchmarks; use your own past launches or a small paid test.

The formulas

  • List buyers = list size x email click rate x sales-page conversion. Paid buyers = (ad budget / cost per click) x paid conversion.
  • Net revenue = gross revenue - refunds - platform and payment fees.
  • Profit = net revenue - ad budget - fixed launch costs - support cost per buyer.
  • Break-even buyers = (ad budget + fixed launch costs) / (price x (1 - refund rate) x (1 - fee rate) - support cost per buyer).

Worked example

A $197 course with $2,400 of fixed launch costs and a $3,000 ad budget puts $5,400 at risk. In the pessimistic case the launch brings 18.7 buyers and a profit of $-2,458. In the base case it brings 48.9 buyers and a profit of $2,639, with a return on ad spend of 3.21 and a paid buyer costing $73. Break-even needs 32.9 buyers.

The decision is not whether the base case is positive. It is whether you can afford the pessimistic loss. Cap the ad budget at an amount you can lose, and write down a stop rule before you spend.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using case-study conversion rates instead of your own.
  • Forgetting refunds and platform fees.
  • Judging success by revenue instead of profit.
