Food truck permits: the 5-permit stack method (2026)

For first-time operators, pre-launch. Free method below; verify every fee with the issuing agency.
Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $59 permit navigator implementing this method (Food Truck Permit Navigator). This guide is the method itself, free to use. Planning information, not legal advice.

1. There is no single "food truck license"

The stack is usually five-plus separate items from unconnected agencies: business license, mobile vendor/health permit, food handler certifications, commissary agreement, fire inspection — plus vehicle registration, seller's permit, and insurance. Miss one and the launch slips weeks or the truck gets shut down mid-shift.

2. Map every permit before spending a dollar

List each item with its agency, fee, prerequisite, and renewal date. Typical fee totals land in the low thousands; timelines run 4–10 weeks. A truck that needs plan review adds weeks more — confirm that before buying or modifying anything.

3. Work the dependency order

Entity and EIN first, then certifications, then commissary agreement, then inspections, then the vendor permit. Some registrations can run in parallel; others need an address or health approval first. Filing everything on the same day is how packets get rejected.

4. Build the renewal system on day one

  1. Record every permit with expiration and 60-day renewal lead time.
  2. Calendar fire-system service, commissary dates, insurance renewals, and credential expirations.
  3. Before changing menu, equipment, commissary, or market, ask which approvals must update.
  4. Keep the document packet (approvals, inspections, agreements, receipts) in one place with an owner and dates.
