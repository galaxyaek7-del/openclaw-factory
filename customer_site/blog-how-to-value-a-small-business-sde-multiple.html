How to Value a Small Business: SDE, Multiples and a Debt-Service Check

The steps behind a first offer on a small business, with a worked example.

Disclosure: Galaxy Forge sells a $49 spreadsheet that implements this method (see the end of the page). This article is complete on its own, and free alternatives exist.

Small businesses are usually priced as a multiple of seller's discretionary earnings (SDE): the pre-tax profit one working owner takes home. The multiplication is easy. Getting SDE honest, choosing a defensible multiple and checking that the price can be paid for are the hard parts. This is not an appraisal; a certified valuation needs a credentialed appraiser.

Step 1: rebuild SDE

SDE = net income + owner salary and payroll taxes + interest + depreciation and amortisation + one-time expenses + owner personal perks - one-time income. Tie each line to the tax returns for three years.

Step 2: choose a multiple range

Multiples vary widely by industry, size, growth and owner dependence. Use comparable recent sales, not the broker's pitch. With an example SDE of $253,000, multiples of 2.0, 2.6 and 3.2 give $506,000, $657,800 and $809,600. Moving the multiple by 0.6 moves the price by about $151,800.

Step 3: cross-check

A discounted cash flow, using a replacement-manager wage, capital spending, tax, growth and a discount rate, gives $771,663 in the example. Net assets (assets sold minus liabilities assumed) are $105,000, a floor rather than a ceiling. If the methods disagree widely, one assumption is carrying the answer.

Step 4: can the business pay for itself?

With 20% down and a 10-year loan at 10.5% on the blended value of $614,732, annual debt service is about $79,631. The cash left after paying a replacement manager is $183,000, a coverage ratio of 2.30. Lenders commonly look for 1.25 or more.

Common mistakes

  • Adding back real, recurring costs as one-time.
  • Forgetting that the owner's job still has to be done by someone.
  • Using a multiple from a business ten times larger.
  • Valuing on the seller's projections instead of the last three years.
