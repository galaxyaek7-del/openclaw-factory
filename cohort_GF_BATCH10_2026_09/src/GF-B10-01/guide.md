## 1. What this kit is and who it serves

This kit serves founders, operations managers, and small teams at US businesses that sell subscriptions, memberships, or any product with automatic renewal. That includes software subscriptions billed monthly or annually, membership programs with recurring dues, box or replenishment programs that ship on a schedule, maintenance plans that renew each year, and service agreements that roll over unless the customer cancels. If money is collected on a recurring basis without the customer taking a fresh purchase action each cycle, the practices in this guide apply to your review. The kit does not assume a particular billing platform. The worksheets describe screens, emails, and checkout elements in platform-neutral terms so a team using common commerce tools, a custom checkout, or a mix of online signup and assisted sales can use the same review structure. The audience is the operator who has to get the work done: the founder who approves the language, the operations manager who runs the audit, the support lead who tests cancellation, and the contractor or assistant who logs state requirements. Each section names the role that normally performs it and the evidence that role should file when finished.

The kit contains five components that work as a set. First, a disclosure checklist that walks each customer touchpoint where renewal terms must be presented clearly before purchase. Second, a consent-flow audit worksheet that traces how the customer agrees to the renewal term and whether that agreement is separate, recorded, and retrievable. Third, a cancellation-path test protocol that has a tester actually attempt to cancel through each channel the business offers and records clicks, time, obstacles, and confirmations. Fourth, a state-law tracker that records which of the roughly 30 state auto-renewal laws you have reviewed with counsel, what each requires in plain operational language, and when each entry was last verified. Fifth, a 30/60/90-day remediation plan that sequences fixes from urgent disclosure gaps through consent evidence improvements to longer-horizon monitoring. The Excel workbook carries all five as separate sheets with formulas that count open items, compute days since last verification, and roll up status. The three Word templates give you a cancellation-path test script, a disclosure review sign-off sheet, and a remediation plan memo for leadership or counsel.

A note on scope discipline. This kit covers disclosure, consent, and cancellation for consumer-facing auto-renewal offers under the federal Restore Online Shoppers Confidence Act, usually called ROSCA, and the collection of state automatic-renewal statutes that now number around 30. It does not cover credit-card processing rules, chargeback dispute tactics, debt collection, telemarketing scripts, or business-to-business contract negotiation. Those topics touch adjacent rules but require different review methods and different counsel. Keeping this kit narrow is deliberate: a team that tries to review everything at once usually finishes nothing. Work the three practices in this guide until each has an owner, a record, and a re-check date, then expand to adjacent topics with the same method.

## 3. Problem background - why auto-renewal practice draws scrutiny

Automatic renewal converts a single purchase decision into an ongoing charge. Regulators at the federal and state level have treated that conversion as a point of elevated responsibility for more than a decade: before charging on a recurring basis, the seller is expected to present the renewal terms clearly, obtain the customer agreement to those terms in a distinct step, and provide a cancellation path that works in practice rather than on paper. ROSCA sets the federal baseline for online transactions, and roughly 30 states maintain their own automatic-renewal statutes that add requirements around font prominence, placement, acknowledgment, reminders, and cancellation methods. Because the state statutes are not identical, a disclosure and cancellation design that satisfies one state review may still leave open questions in another, which is why the tracker in this kit treats each state as a separate verification task instead of assuming one review covers all states.

Enforcement activity between 2024 and 2026 included several multi-million-dollar settlements involving subscription disclosure, consent, and cancellation practices. This guide does not recount the details of any specific matter, because settlement outcomes turn on their own facts and counsel should explain which facts resemble your situation. The operational lesson operators should take from that period is general and durable: enforcers examined the full customer journey rather than a single page, comparing what the checkout page showed, what the confirmation email restated, how the consent record read, and how hard cancellation proved to be when a tester tried it. Gaps that looked minor in isolation, such as renewal language placed below the purchase button, a pre-checked box standing in for explicit agreement, or a cancellation path that required a phone call during narrow hours while signup took one click online, drew attention when viewed together as a pattern. This kit therefore reviews the journey end to end and asks you to keep the evidence for each stage in one place.

In March 2026 the Federal Trade Commission issued an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, often shortened to ANPRM, seeking comment on whether to revive a federal rule addressing automatic renewals and related practices. An ANPRM is an early-stage request for public input, not a final rule, and it does not by itself change what any business must do today. Its practical meaning for operators is that federal expectations in this area may move again and that practices built only around the narrowest reading of current text may need revision if a rule issues. The sound response is not to guess the content of a future rule but to build review habits that adapt: clear disclosure language you can change in one place, a consent record you can retrieve per customer, a cancellation path you test on a schedule, and a dated log showing when each was last checked. Those four habits satisfy current review needs and leave you positioned to respond if federal or state text changes. Your counsel can advise how closely to monitor the ANPRM docket for your offer type.

State law remains the largest source of variation. Around 30 states have automatic-renewal statutes, and legislatures amend them on their own schedules. Some states emphasize reminder notices before a renewal charge after a trial or before an annual term. Some specify that certain cancellation methods must be available. Some address free-trial conversions with distinct language. Because amendment cycles do not align, a tracker entry that was accurate last year may be incomplete today, which is why every row in the State-Law Register carries a last-verified date and a verification source, and why the workbook flags entries older than a threshold you set. The instruction throughout this kit is consistent: verify state law locally for each state where you have customers, record who verified it and when, and re-verify on a schedule rather than treating any summary as permanent. No summary table in any commercial kit, including this one, substitutes for that verification step with qualified counsel.

## 4. Concepts you will use throughout the kit

Disclosure means the renewal terms presented to the customer before purchase in a form the customer can notice, read, and keep. Operationally, reviewers check four attributes: content completeness, which asks whether price, billing frequency, renewal timing, trial-conversion terms where applicable, and cancellation method are all stated; prominence, which asks whether the language is visible without scrolling past it or hunting for it; proximity, which asks whether the terms sit near the action that creates the charge rather than on a distant page; and retainability, which asks whether the customer receives or can retrieve the terms after purchase, typically in a confirmation message and an account record. A disclosure can fail on any one attribute while passing the other three, so the checklist scores each attribute separately instead of giving a single pass mark for the page.

Consent means the customer affirmative agreement to the renewal term, captured as a distinct step and stored so it can be retrieved later. Reviewers distinguish an explicit step, such as checking an unchecked box labeled with the renewal terms or clicking a button whose adjacent language states the renewal charge, from a passive arrangement such as a pre-checked box or agreement inferred from completing the purchase alone. The worksheet asks whether the step is separate from general terms acceptance, whether the language adjacent to the action states the recurring nature of the charge, whether the record stored includes the version of the terms, the timestamp, and the channel, and whether support staff can retrieve that record when a customer disputes a charge. Teams often discover that their platform stores an order timestamp but not the terms version the customer saw, which makes later disputes difficult to resolve from records alone.

Cancellation means the path by which a customer stops future recurring charges, measured as the customer experiences it rather than as the procedure manual describes it. Reviewers test each offered channel end to end: the online path inside the account portal, any phone path including hours and hold time, any email or message path including response time, and any chat or assisted path. For each channel the tester records the starting point, the number of steps or clicks, the elapsed time, any required retention offers or surveys and whether they can be skipped, the confirmation received, and whether billing records confirm that no further charge followed. A path that is simple on paper but requires the customer to discover a hidden menu, complete a mandatory exit survey, or call during limited hours after signing up online in one click will score poorly on the customer-experience measures even if each individual step seems reasonable to the business.

Two supporting ideas recur in every section. The first is evidence retention: for each claim about what customers see, keep the dated screenshot, the language version, and the tester notes together so a later reviewer can reconstruct what existed on a given date. The second is version control for language: every disclosure sentence, consent label, and confirmation message should carry a version identifier and an effective date in your internal log, even if customers never see that identifier. When language changes, the log shows which customers saw which version, which is the minimum record needed to answer questions about past transactions. The workbook implements both ideas with columns for evidence links, version identifiers, and dates, plus formulas that surface missing evidence and stale verifications automatically.

## 5. Method overview - the audit loop in five passes

The kit organizes the work as five passes that can be completed in sequence or assigned to different owners in parallel. Pass one maps every enrollment surface: each checkout page, plan selector, trial offer, phone script, and in-person form through which a customer can enter a recurring charge. Pass two audits disclosure at each surface against the checklist, scoring content, prominence, proximity, and retainability separately. Pass three audits the consent step at each surface, verifying separation, language adjacency, record completeness, and retrievability. Pass four tests each cancellation channel end to end with timed trials and confirmation checks. Pass five consolidates findings into the state-law review package and the 30/60/90-day remediation plan. Each pass has a workbook sheet, a completion criterion, and a sign-off row so the team knows when a pass is genuinely finished rather than informally discussed.

- Pass 1 - Map surfaces: list every URL, script, and form that can create a recurring charge, with owner and last review date.

- Pass 2 - Disclosure audit: score each surface on content, prominence, proximity, and retainability; file screenshots.

- Pass 3 - Consent audit: verify the agreement step is distinct, labeled, timestamped, versioned, and retrievable per customer.

- Pass 4 - Cancellation testing: time and log each channel end to end, confirm the charge stops, file the confirmation.

- Pass 5 - Consolidate: package findings for counsel review, log state verifications, and sequence fixes in the remediation plan.

Run the full loop quarterly and a shortened loop monthly. The monthly check re-tests cancellation timing on the highest-volume channel, confirms no unlogged language change occurred, and reviews any customer complaints mentioning renewal, cancellation, or trial conversion. The quarterly loop repeats all five passes and refreshes the state-law verification dates for the states holding your largest customer counts. The workbook Remediation Tracker computes days open per item and flags items past due so the cadence enforces itself once owners and dates are entered. If a platform migration, checkout redesign, or pricing change occurs between scheduled loops, run passes two through four immediately for the affected surface rather than waiting for the next cycle, because redesigns are the most common source of new disclosure gaps.

## 6. Workflow A - disclosure checklist, touchpoint by touchpoint

Begin with an inventory of every point where renewal terms reach the customer. For an online offer the standard touchpoints are the plan-selection page, the checkout or payment page, any order-bump or upsell step that adds a recurring item, the confirmation page shown after payment, the confirmation email or receipt, the account or billing-settings screen, any pre-renewal reminder message, and any trial-conversion notice. For assisted sales add the spoken script, any recorded confirmation, and any paper or electronic form the customer signs. Enter each touchpoint as one row in the Disclosure Audit Log with the address or script name, the owner, and the language version in effect. Attach or link the dated screenshot or script excerpt in the evidence column. A common early finding is that the confirmation email restates the amount but omits the renewal timing or the cancellation method, which breaks retainability even when the checkout page itself is complete.

Score each touchpoint against the disclosure checklist using the four attributes defined in Section 4. Content completeness asks whether the row states the recurring amount, the billing frequency, the date or trigger of the next charge, the length of any trial and what happens when it ends, and the cancellation method in terms a new customer can follow. Prominence asks whether the renewal terms appear in type and contrast comparable to the price itself, without requiring the customer to open a collapsed section, follow a link, or scroll past the purchase button to find them. Proximity asks whether the terms sit immediately above or beside the button or signature step that creates the charge. Retainability asks whether the customer can retrieve the same terms after purchase without contacting support. Score each attribute as present, partial, or missing, and require a note for any partial or missing score describing exactly what the customer sees instead. The workbook counts missing and partial items per touchpoint and rolls them into a touchpoint status that stays open until every attribute scores present or counsel has recorded an accepted alternative.

Work through checkout variants separately, because plan selectors, coupon codes, and trial toggles can change which language renders. Test the monthly and annual plans as separate rows, test with and without a trial where both exist, and test on both desktop and mobile layouts if a material share of signups arrives by phone. Record the exact language version for each variant, since a fix applied to the desktop template sometimes fails to propagate to the mobile template or to an older hosted page still receiving traffic from email links. When a gap is found, write the corrective sentence in plain language in the finding note, assign an owner, and link the finding to a remediation item rather than editing the live page from inside the audit session. The audit pass documents reality; the remediation pass changes it under control with counsel review of the new language before it ships.

Close the disclosure pass with the sign-off template. The reviewer signs that each listed surface was inspected on the stated date, that screenshots and language copies are filed, that each gap has a remediation entry, and that the package is ready for counsel review. File the signed sheet with the evidence set. If counsel directs language changes, record the new version identifier and effective date in the log and re-score the affected rows rather than overwriting the original scores, so the history shows what changed and when. Illustrative example: a fictional company used here only as an illustration, labeled illustrative throughout, finds that its annual plan page states the yearly amount beside the purchase button but places the renewal-date sentence three screens below in a help link; the checklist scores content as partial and proximity as missing, the finding note quotes the two placements, and the remediation item proposes moving a one-sentence renewal statement adjacent to the button for counsel review. The illustration shows the method, not a legal conclusion about any real page.

## 7. Workflow B - consent-flow audit worksheet

The consent audit starts where disclosure ends: assuming the terms are visible, did the customer take a distinct action agreeing to the recurring charge, and can you prove it later. List each enrollment surface from the disclosure pass as a row in the Consent-Flow Checklist sheet and answer the same six questions for each. First, is there a separate agreement step for the renewal term apart from general terms acceptance, such as an unchecked box or an explicit button label tied to the recurring charge. Second, does the language immediately adjacent to that step state that the charge recurs, at what amount and frequency. Third, does the step avoid pre-checked boxes or implied agreement standing alone. Fourth, does the stored record include the customer identifier, the timestamp, the channel, and the terms version. Fifth, can a support agent retrieve that record within a single customer interaction. Sixth, does the confirmation message restate the recurring nature of the charge rather than describing the transaction as a one-time purchase. Any negative answer becomes a finding with an owner and a remediation link.

Pay close attention to platform defaults, because many commerce tools ship with general-terms acceptance built in and leave renewal-specific consent for the merchant to configure. Export a real test order and inspect what the platform stored: the order timestamp alone is not a consent record. If the platform cannot store the terms version natively, create a controlled workaround such as a versioned language log keyed by date range, so any order timestamp maps to exactly one terms version. Document the workaround in the worksheet notes and have counsel confirm whether it meets the evidentiary need for your states. Where phone or assisted sales create recurring charges, the consent evidence is the script plus the recording or signed form; verify that the script contains the renewal terms verbatim, that agents read them rather than paraphrasing, and that recordings or forms are retained under the same schedule as online records. Illustrative example, labeled illustrative: a fictional online seller discovers its checkout records the order total and timestamp but has no field for the terms version, so it adds a dated language-version table and a nightly order-to-version mapping, then re-tests retrieval with two sample orders from different weeks. The illustration demonstrates record reconstruction, not a compliance determination.

Re-test consent after every checkout change, however small. Plan-selector redesigns, one-click payment additions, and express-checkout buttons can all bypass or reorder the consent step without anyone intending it. Add a consent re-check to the deployment checklist for any change touching the purchase path: place a test order, verify the separate step still renders, confirm the record stores the new version, and retrieve it as support would. Log the re-check as a new row rather than editing the old one, preserving the history. The workbook formula that counts negative answers per surface gives you a single number to watch across releases; when it rises after a deploy, the team knows the change introduced a consent regression before customers report it.

## 8. Workflow C - cancellation-path test protocol

Cancellation testing is the most hands-on pass and the one teams postpone most often, which is why this kit makes it a scripted, timed exercise with a template. For each channel the business offers or implies, a tester who did not design the flow follows the written test script, starts a timer when searching for how to cancel begins, and records every step until a confirmation arrives. The standard channels to test are the self-service online path inside the account, any phone path with its hours and hold time, any email or support-message path with its response time, and any chat path. If signup is available online in one click, test whether cancellation is available online as well and whether the effort is comparable; where channels differ, the test record should state the difference plainly so leadership and counsel see it. Test as both a logged-in customer and, where applicable, a customer who lost account access, because the locked-out path is where many real complaints originate.

The test script template structures each trial identically: objective, starting point, step-by-step actions with timestamps, obstacles encountered, retention offers or surveys and whether skipping was possible, confirmation details, and a follow-up billing check. Obstacles to record include menus that hide cancellation under unrelated labels, mandatory exit surveys that block progress, repeated retention offers with no visible skip, requirements to call during limited hours, requirements to write to a postal address, and any error that forces a restart. For each obstacle, the tester copies the exact on-screen wording and takes a screenshot, because paraphrase loses the detail counsel needs. After the trial, verify in billing records or a test transaction report that no further recurring charge is scheduled, and file the confirmation alongside the trial notes. A cancellation that shows a success screen but leaves a future charge scheduled is recorded as a failure, not a pass.

Score each channel on five measures: discoverability, meaning time from account home to the start of cancellation; step count, meaning clicks or actions to completion; elapsed time including holds and waits; skippability of non-essential steps; and confirmation quality, meaning a dated, retrievable confirmation stating that future recurring charges stop and on what date. The workbook Cancellation-Path Test Log computes average elapsed time per channel across trials and flags channels with no passing trial in the current quarter. Run at least two trials per channel per quarter with different testers to reduce familiarity bias, and rotate one trial to the locked-out scenario. Illustrative example, labeled illustrative: a fictional membership seller times its online path at four minutes across three clicks with an emailed confirmation, while its phone path averages twenty-two minutes including hold time and yields only a verbal confirmation with no written follow-up; the log shows the contrast numerically, the finding proposes adding written confirmation to the phone path and re-testing, and counsel reviews whether channel asymmetry raises questions under the states being tracked. The numbers are invented for illustration of the method and describe no real business.

Handle retention offers with care during testing. The protocol does not forbid offering a discount or pause; it requires that any offer be skippable without confusion, that declining the offer advances cancellation rather than restarting it, and that the final confirmation is unambiguous. Record the exact offer language, the number of offer screens, and whether the decline control is labeled as clearly as the accept control. If a tester cannot determine whether cancellation completed after declining an offer, the trial is a failure and the finding should say so directly. File all trial records with the quarter label so trends across quarters are visible: a channel whose elapsed time doubles after a redesign has regressed even if no single step looks unreasonable in isolation.

## 9. Workflow D - state-law tracker, verification discipline

The State-Law Register is an index of verification work, not a statement of what any state requires. Each row names one state, the date it was last verified, who verified it with counsel, the operational requirements counsel confirmed in plain language, the disclosure and cancellation rows those requirements map to, and the next re-verification date. Enter a row for every state where you have customers, plus any state where you actively market even without current customers, since marketing reach can raise review questions before the first sale. Leave the requirement cells empty until verification occurs; the workbook marks empty-requirement rows as pending and excludes them from any rollup that could be mistaken for coverage. Never copy requirements from one state row to another, and never treat a prior-year entry as current without a fresh verification date. The discipline the tracker enforces is simple: no date and no source means no reliance.

Set re-verification intervals by customer concentration and amendment activity. States holding your largest customer counts merit review each quarter alongside the full audit loop; remaining states merit review at least annually or promptly when counsel flags an amendment. The workbook computes days since verification per row and flags rows past your threshold, so stale entries surface without manual review. When counsel confirms a requirement, phrase the row in operational terms the team can execute, such as which reminder notice to send and when, which cancellation method to maintain, or which acknowledgment record to store, and link the row to the corresponding disclosure, consent, or cancellation evidence. When counsel identifies an open question, record it as an open question with a due date rather than resolving it by assumption. ## 10. Workflow E - the 30/60/90-day remediation plan

Consolidate every finding from the four passes into the Remediation Tracker with a severity, an owner, a due date, and a counsel-review flag. Severity in this kit is operational, not legal: urgent means customers currently see incomplete renewal terms, no distinct consent record exists, or a cancellation channel fails end to end; near-term means records are incomplete or confirmations are weak but the customer-facing path functions; longer-horizon means monitoring, training, and re-verification habits. Assign each urgent item to the 30-day window, each near-term item to the 60-day window, and each longer-horizon item to the 90-day window, then review capacity honestly: if the 30-day window holds more work than the team can ship with counsel review, escalate scope or sequencing rather than skipping the review step. The workbook rolls up counts per window, counts past-due items, and computes days open per row so the plan status is visible at a glance.

Sequence the work so disclosure fixes precede consent-record fixes, which precede monitoring improvements, because each layer depends on the one before it. In days 1 to 30, correct incomplete or poorly placed renewal language on the highest-traffic surfaces, add missing cancellation confirmations, and fix any channel that fails end to end; route every new customer-facing sentence through counsel before it ships and log the new version with its effective date. In days 31 to 60, close consent-record gaps by adding terms-version storage and retrieval, align confirmation messages so they restate the recurring charge, and retrain support staff on retrieving consent records and processing cancellations without added friction. In days 61 to 90, refresh state verifications for high-concentration states, schedule the quarterly test cadence in the team calendar, and run the full loop once more to confirm the fixes held. Each completed item requires attached evidence, a re-test row, and a sign-off before it closes; items closed without evidence reopen automatically at the next review.

Report progress weekly on one page: items closed with evidence, items past due with cause, new findings since last week, and questions awaiting counsel. The remediation memo template provides that one-page structure plus a plain-language summary for leadership that describes what changed for the customer, not just what changed in the code. Illustrative example, labeled illustrative: a fictional seller enters the plan with nine findings, places three disclosure corrections and one cancellation-confirmation fix in the 30-day window, four consent-record and training items in the 60-day window, and two state re-verification and scheduling items in the 90-day window; at day 30 it has closed three of four with filed evidence while the fourth awaits counsel language review, so the report shows the delay and the interim mitigation rather than marking the item complete. ## 11. Roles, cadence, and document control

Assign four roles even on a small team, combining where needed but naming each explicitly. The disclosure owner maintains the language log and the audit sheet. The consent owner maintains the worksheet and the retrieval test. The cancellation owner schedules testers and files trial records. The tracker owner maintains verification dates and the counsel question list. One person may hold two roles, but no role sits vacant, and the remediation owner, usually the founder or operations manager, runs the weekly one-page review. Record the assignment in the workbook notes row of each sheet so a new team member can see who owns what without asking around.

Operate on two cadences. Monthly, re-test the highest-volume cancellation channel, confirm no unlogged language change shipped, and review complaints mentioning renewal, trial, or cancellation. Quarterly, run all five passes, refresh high-concentration state verifications, and file a signed package. After any checkout redesign, pricing change, platform migration, or trial-term change, run the affected passes immediately. Keep every artifact under version control by effective date: disclosure language versions, consent-step screenshots, trial records by quarter, tracker verifications by date, and remediation evidence by item. When an enforcer, payment partner, or counsel asks what existed on a given date, the dated file set should answer without reconstruction.

Control the documents with simple rules. Store screenshots and language copies in a dated folder per quarter. Name files with the surface, the date, and the version identifier so sorting by name sorts by time. Never edit a filed trial record; add a new row for re-tests. Never overwrite a tracker verification; add a new date and source. The workbook formulas that count missing evidence and stale verifications are only as honest as the data entry behind them, so treat empty cells as open work rather than filling them with optimistic text. A tracker that shows open items is a working tool; a tracker that shows everything complete without filed evidence is a warning sign.

## 13. Frequently asked questions

Does this kit replace legal counsel. No. It organizes facts, evidence, and questions so counsel can advise efficiently. Every state row, every new disclosure sentence, and every open interpretation question should pass through qualified counsel, and the tracker records when that happened.

We sell in only a few states. Do we still need the tracker. Yes, but the workload scales with reach. Enter the states where you have customers, verify those first, and expand as marketing reach grows. Re-verify on schedule because amendment cycles do not pause for small sellers.

Our billing platform handles subscriptions. Are we covered. Not automatically. Platforms provide tools; the merchant configures language, consent steps, reminders, and cancellation paths. Export a real test order, inspect what was stored, run the cancellation test, and log what you find rather than assuming platform defaults answer every question.

What is the single most common gap teams find. In illustrative walkthroughs used to design this kit, the frequent pattern was a complete checkout page paired with an incomplete confirmation message or a consent step that looked distinct but stored no retrievable version. Your results will differ; the method finds your gaps rather than predicting them.

How do trial offers change the review. Trials add a conversion event that needs its own disclosure, its own reminder discipline where counsel advises it, and its own test path. Log trial and non-trial variants as separate rows through disclosure, consent, and cancellation rather than reviewing one variant and assuming the other matches.

How should we handle phone or assisted sales. Script the renewal terms verbatim, train agents to read them rather than paraphrase, retain the recording or signed form on the same schedule as online records, and test the assisted cancellation path with the same timing and confirmation discipline as the online path.

What if our cancellation requires a call while signup is online. Record the asymmetry exactly as measured, carry it to counsel promptly, and treat written confirmation plus billing verification as minimum interim controls while the design question is open. Do not describe the paths as equivalent in customer-facing language if the measured effort differs materially.

How often should state entries be re-verified. Quarterly for states holding your largest customer counts and at least annually for the rest, plus promptly when counsel flags an amendment. The workbook flags stale entries automatically once you set the threshold.

What records should we keep and for how long. Keep dated screenshots, language versions with effective dates, consent records per customer, trial logs with confirmations, tracker verifications with sources, and remediation evidence. Retention periods depend on the states and forums involved, so set the schedule with counsel and record the schedule itself in the tracker notes.

Can this kit promise that our practices satisfy any law. No, and it does not attempt to. It documents current practice, structures verification with counsel, and tracks remediation with evidence. Any statement about legal sufficiency must come from qualified counsel, not from a worksheet formula.

What should we do if the federal ANPRM becomes a proposed rule. Ask counsel to assess the proposal against your logged surfaces, keep the audit loop running so your evidence is current, and avoid redesigning flows around press summaries of the proposal before counsel has reviewed the actual text.

How do we start if the team is only one person. Run the two-hour quick start, then work one workflow per week in the order disclosure, consent, cancellation, tracker, remediation. File evidence as you go so each week hands the next week a complete input.
