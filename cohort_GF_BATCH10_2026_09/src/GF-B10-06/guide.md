# DSAR Response Kit - Complete Guide

## Introduction: who this kit serves and what it organizes

This guide serves small software teams of 2 to 20 people with no legal operations function, including founders, support leads, and engineers who handle data subject access and deletion requests alongside daily work. It organizes the handling of access requests and deletion requests through a 5-stage state machine: Received, Identity verified, Scoped, Fulfilled, Closed. It applies a 23-business-day internal service target that sits inside the statutory clocks summarized in the brief: 30 days under GDPR and 45 days under several US state laws. It provides a data-source registry, a deletion runbook, and response templates.

Not legal advice, verify obligations with qualified counsel. That sentence governs the entire kit. Nothing in the guide, workbook, or templates determines which law applies to any request, which clock runs, which exemption holds, or which retention basis is valid. Counsel makes those determinations per matter. The kit organizes intake, verification, scoping, fulfillment, and records so that counsel advice can be applied on time and shown afterward.

The kit contents include an intake log with service-date formulas, an identity-verification checklist, a data-source registry, a deletion runbook, and four response letter templates covering access fulfilled, deletion confirmed, rejection with basis, and extension notice. The workbook contains five sheets: request log with deadline formulas, verification checklist, source registry, deletion runbook tracker, and service dashboard. The templates package contains five documents: intake form fields plus the four letters.

This guide contains problem background, the step-by-step method, stage workflows, checklists, worked illustrative examples labeled illustrative, frequently asked questions, limitations, and a disclosure section. All request scenarios, requester details, dates, reference numbers, system names, and letter contents in examples are illustrative. They demonstrate how to use the forms, not real requests or real legal conclusions. Fixed product facts from the brief are limited to the 5-stage workflow, the 23-business-day internal target, the 30-day GDPR clock summary, the 45-day several-US-states clock summary, the four response letters, and the price of 59 dollars. Every other number appears inside an illustrative example or as a structural step number. No regulatory outcome, deadline determination, or approval is promised at any point.

How to use this guide: read it once end to end, list real systems in the source registry, then handle each live request in stage order without skipping verification. Log on receipt, verify identity before searching, agree scope, fulfill per the runbook, send the matching letter after counsel review where required, and close with a filed record. Orderly handling repeated per request is the entire method.

## 1. Problem background: why small teams miss request clocks

Access and deletion requests arrive as ordinary messages. A requester writes to support asking for a copy of personal data or for deletion. The message looks like a support ticket, so it enters the support queue without a clock, a stage, or an owner. Days pass while the team handles outages, releases, and customer issues. When someone recognizes the message as a formal request, part of the clock has already run. The remaining time compresses verification, search, review, and response into a rushed sequence where mistakes become likely.

Three structural gaps cause most misses. First, no registry: requests live in individual inboxes, so nobody sees the full queue, the oldest item, or the nearest due date. Second, no verification discipline: the team searches systems or shares data before confirming the requester controls the account, creating disclosure and fraud exposure. Third, no audit trail: work happens across chat, email, and database tools without a dated record of what was searched, what was deleted, which counsel advice was followed, and which letter was sent. When questions arise later, the team cannot reconstruct the handling from memory.

Small size sharpens each gap. Two to 20 people means the same engineer who ships features also runs database deletions, and the same support lead who answers tickets also drafts response letters. Context switching buries request work. Knowledge concentrates in one or two people, so absence stalls handling. No legal operations function means nobody owns deadline interpretation, exemption analysis, or retention decisions as a standing role. The kit answers these conditions with a narrow, repeatable routine rather than a large program: one log, one stage order, one verification checklist, one registry of sources, one runbook per deletion, one letter per outcome, each with counsel-review points marked.

Clock pressure deserves plain explanation. The brief summarizes the external clocks as 30 days under GDPR and 45 days under several US state laws. Real clocks vary by jurisdiction, request type, amendments, and counsel interpretation, which is why every deadline field in the kit is a working aid subject to counsel confirmation rather than a legal determination. The kit computes a 30 business-day proxy date and a 23-business-day internal target date from receipt in the request log. The internal target creates working margin: verification and scoping finish early enough that fulfillment, review, and sending still fit inside the external clock counsel confirms. Margin is operational, not legal. It does not extend, pause, or satisfy any statutory duty. Only timely, correct handling under counsel advice does that.

Deletion handling carries its own background risks. Personal data in a small SaaS product typically spreads across a primary database, authentication records, support tickets, logs, analytics stores, backups, and third-party processors such as email delivery or payment handling. Deleting the profile row while leaving support history, logs, and processor copies untouched produces an incomplete fulfillment that the record cannot defend. Backup rotation adds timing complexity because restoration copies expire on schedule rather than on demand. Log retention for security and debugging may have a lawful basis counsel must assess. The source registry and runbook tracker exist to make this spread visible per request and to record per-source actions with evidence, rather than treating deletion as a single database statement.

## 2. Method overview: the 5-stage state machine and the internal target

The state machine has five stages in fixed order. Received means the request is logged with its original text preserved and an acknowledgment sent. Identity verified means the verification checklist is complete and the requester is confirmed as the account holder or an authorized agent to the standard counsel requires. Scoped means the team and, where needed, counsel have fixed the search scope: which systems, which time period, which data categories, and which exclusions or exemptions apply. Fulfilled means the access package is prepared or the deletion steps are executed per the runbook, reviewed, and the response letter sent. Closed means the record is complete and filed: log entries, verification evidence, scope note, per-source fulfillment notes, counsel advice references, letter copy, and dates.

Each stage has entry and exit criteria. Received exits when the log entry exists, the original text is preserved, and acknowledgment is sent. Identity verified exits when every checklist item passes and the verifier records name, date, and evidence reference. Scoped exits when the scope note names systems, categories, period, and any counsel-directed exclusions. Fulfilled exits when the package or deletions are complete, reviewed by a second person where staffing allows, and the letter is sent. Closed exits when the file contains every dated artifact and the log status reads Closed. No stage is skipped. In particular, search never starts before verification passes, because pre-verification search both wastes effort on potentially invalid requests and creates unnecessary exposure of personal data.

The 23-business-day internal target structures pacing. Measured from receipt using business-day arithmetic in the workbook, it marks the date by which fulfillment should be ready for review and sending, leaving margin before the external clock counsel confirms. When the internal date approaches with verification still open, the team escalates the same day rather than letting the request drift. When scope proves wide, the team seeks counsel advice on extension or phased handling early, since extension notices generally must go out before the original deadline passes where the applicable law permits extensions. The dashboard sheet surfaces requests due within a short illustrative threshold and requests past the working proxy date so attention goes to the oldest and nearest items first.

Roles fit small teams. One person owns the log and the clock per request, often the support lead. One person owns verification evidence. One person, often an engineer, owns source searches and deletion steps. The founder or a designated lead owns counsel contact and letter approval. Where the team has only two people, one person may hold several roles, but the stage order and the written record do not collapse: the same person still completes verification before search and still files each artifact. Separation of review, where a second person checks the access package or deletion evidence before sending, is applied whenever staffing allows and is noted in the record when it is not possible.

## 3. Intake and logging: preserving the request on day one

Intake begins the moment a message arrives that asks for personal data or deletion, however informally phrased. Treat borderline messages as potential requests: log first, then assess with counsel guidance. The intake form fields template records reference number in illustrative form, date and time received, channel, requester name as given, contact for verification, request type, whether the original text is attached, and who received it. Preserve the original wording exactly. Paraphrase loses the scope signals and the date evidence the record needs.

Log the request in the request log sheet the same day. Columns capture reference, receipt date, type, contact, verification flag, scope-agreed date, computed statutory proxy date, computed internal target date, stage, computed days remaining, and status. Two formulas drive the sheet: the proxy date adds 30 business days to receipt and the internal date adds 23 business days to receipt using business-day functions, while days remaining counts business days from today to the proxy date. These are working computations, not legal determinations. Counsel confirms the real clock per matter, including which receipt date counts, which days count, and whether amendments or clarifications affect timing. When counsel sets a different date, record it in the log as the governing date and keep the computed columns as a cross-check rather than overwriting their formulas.

Send an acknowledgment promptly that confirms receipt, states the reference, and explains the next steps of verification without disclosing any personal data. The acknowledgment is not a scope agreement and not a fulfillment. It starts the relationship record and stops the requester wondering whether the message arrived. File the sent acknowledgment with the request record and advance the log stage to Received with status Open.

Triage the same day for urgency signals that require immediate counsel contact: requests involving children, sensitive categories, disputes, threats of complaints to authorities, or requests from someone other than the account holder. Triage does not change stage order. It changes how quickly counsel is consulted and how closely the log owner watches the internal date. Note the triage decision and its date in the record.

## 4. Identity verification: confirming the requester before any search

Verification answers one question: does this requester control the account or otherwise hold authority to receive this data or direct its deletion, to the standard counsel requires. The verification checklist sheet records one row per check with request reference in illustrative form, check description, evidence seen, verifier name, date, and result. Typical checks include matching a government-issued identifier to the account name where counsel approves that method, confirming control of the account email through a verification link, confirming account-specific facts that only the holder would know, and validating agent authorization where a third party acts for the requester. Minimize copies of identity documents. Note a reference to the evidence rather than storing full copies where possible, and follow counsel guidance on retention of verification materials.

Do not search systems broadly before verification passes, except for the minimum lookup needed to confirm the account exists and to route the request. Broad pre-verification search creates copies, logs, and access events around personal data for a request that may prove invalid. It also starts effort the team may need to discard. The single allowed early step is existence confirmation through the narrowest query available, recorded with date and operator.

Handle failed verification plainly. When the requester cannot complete the required checks, record the attempts with dates, explain in writing what remains needed, and keep the stage at Received. Do not advance to Scoped. Do not disclose data. Do not delete data on unverified instruction, since deletion on a forged request destroys a real person's account. Set a follow-up date and note it in the log. When follow-up expires under the process counsel approved, record the expiry and seek counsel advice on closure or extension handling rather than inventing a rejection basis.

Handle agent and third-party requests with extra care. Require written authorization that counsel has approved as sufficient, verify the agent's own identity, and confirm the scope of authority before proceeding. Log each document with date received. Where authority is unclear, pause and consult counsel with the documents attached rather than proceeding on assumption.

## 5. Scoping: fixing systems, categories, period, and exclusions

Scoping converts a general request into a bounded work order. The scope note names the systems to be searched from the source registry, the data categories in play, the time period covered, the requester's clarifications where obtained, and any exclusions or exemptions applied strictly on counsel advice. Scope is agreed in the sense that the team records what it will do and, where the process calls for requester clarification, what the requester confirmed. It is not a negotiation of rights. Where the requester disputes scope, consult counsel and record the advice followed.

Build scope from the registry. The source registry sheet lists each system that may hold requester data with owner, data categories, retention basis, deletion method, and last review date. At scoping time, mark which sources apply to this request and which do not, with a one-line reason for each exclusion such as no requester data held or out of period. Systems commonly include the primary database, authentication store, support desk, logs, analytics, file storage, backups, and named third-party processors. Review the registry whenever systems change so scoping works from a current map rather than memory.

Clarify ambiguous requests early and in writing. When the request does not state a period, an account identifier, or whether deletion or access is wanted, ask focused questions that allow scoping to proceed. Record the questions sent, the answers received, and the dates. Counsel should confirm whether clarification pauses or affects any clock under the applicable law, since rules differ and the kit makes no determination on that point. Never treat a clarification exchange as a reason to stop work without counsel direction.

Record exemption and retention decisions only as counsel advice applied, never as team conclusions. When counsel directs that a category be withheld or retained on a stated basis, record the basis reference, the categories affected, the date of advice, and who gave it. The rejection-with-basis and deletion-confirmed letters carry matching fields so the letter reflects the recorded advice exactly. Consistency between the internal note and the sent letter is part of the audit trail.

## 6. Fulfillment for access: preparing a correct, reviewable package

Access fulfillment collects the scoped data into a reviewable package, checks it, and sends it through an appropriate channel with the access-fulfilled letter. Collection follows the scope note source by source. Each source extraction notes operator, date, query or export method at a general level, and record counts in illustrative form where the record needs completeness evidence. Avoid collecting beyond scope. Over-collection expands exposure and review burden without helping the requester.

Review the package before sending with attention to four checks. Completeness: every scoped source contributed or has a noted reason for absence. Identity match: records pertain to the verified requester, not to similarly named accounts. Third-party data: information about other individuals is handled strictly per counsel direction, since access to one person does not create access to another. Format and channel: the package uses a readable format and a delivery channel proportionate to sensitivity, with access controls where counsel or policy requires them. A second person reviews where staffing allows. Record reviewer, date, and outcome.

The access-fulfilled letter states the request date and agreed scope, describes what is provided and the period covered, notes items withheld with the basis as advised by counsel, and explains how to ask follow-up questions. Fill the template fields with the real reference, scope, materials list, and follow-up contact. Have counsel review the withholding language before sending where the matter involves any exemption. File the sent letter with the package manifest and update the log stage to Fulfilled with the sent date.

Handle large or complex packages by phasing only on counsel advice. Where phasing is used, each phase states what it contains, what remains, and the expected date of the remainder in illustrative scheduling language tied to real dates. Log each send separately so the record shows a complete sequence rather than a single vague fulfillment.

## 7. Fulfillment for deletion: running the runbook source by source

Deletion fulfillment executes the deletion runbook tracker row by row. Each row names the request reference in illustrative form, the source, the action required, the assignee, the due date, the evidence of completion, and whether done. Actions are source-specific: profile and content rows in the primary database, ticket anonymization in the support desk, key rotation or token revocation in authentication, expiry handling in logs and analytics, rotation-based expiry in backups, and processor requests through each vendor channel. Evidence is a ticket number, a log reference, a screenshot reference, or a vendor confirmation reference with date, not a narrative assurance.

Treat backups, logs, and processors as the three areas where small teams most often under-deliver. Backups generally expire on rotation rather than by surgical deletion, so the record states the rotation facts and the date by which the backup containing the data expires, in real dates once known. Logs may carry security or debugging retention that counsel must assess before any deletion, so the record carries the counsel-directed outcome per log class. Processors require per-vendor requests with confirmation references filed, since deleting locally while a processor retains a copy leaves the fulfillment incomplete. The runbook tracker keeps these three areas visible on every deletion so none is forgotten.

Sequence deletion work to protect verification and scope integrity. Confirm verification is complete, confirm scope is fixed, execute per-source actions, collect evidence, run a second-person check where staffing allows, then draft the deletion-confirmed letter. The letter states the request and deletion scope, lists sources addressed at a general level with completion dates, notes lawful retention exceptions exactly as advised by counsel, and describes backup and log handling in plain terms drawn from the runbook. Counsel reviews exception language before sending. File the sent letter with per-source evidence and update the log stage to Fulfilled with the sent date.

Handle partial deletion plainly. Where counsel directs retention of specific categories on a stated basis, the letter and the internal note state the same basis and the same categories. Where a source cannot be deleted for technical reasons within the clock, consult counsel immediately about extension handling or interim measures rather than silently omitting the source. The record shows what was done, what was retained on advice, and what remains with dates, which is the most the operational layer can honestly provide.

## 8. Extensions and rejections: narrow paths with counsel review

Extensions and rejections are narrow, counsel-gated paths, not routine tools. The extension-notice letter references the original request and the clock that applies per counsel advice, explains in plain terms why added time is needed, and gives a new expected response date tied to a real calendar date once counsel sets it. Send the notice before the original deadline passes where the applicable law permits extensions. Record the sent date and file the notice with the request record. Never assume an extension is available. Availability, length, and notice requirements depend on the applicable law and the matter, which counsel determines.

Rejections follow the same gating with stricter review. The rejection-with-basis letter acknowledges the request and the review performed, states the specific basis for declining fully or partly exactly as advised by counsel, and explains available next steps or appeal channels. The template carries an explicit warning not to use it without counsel review of the basis in the specific matter. File the counsel advice reference with the sent letter. A rejection whose basis is invented by the team, however plausible it sounds, creates exposure the kit is designed to prevent. No template language cures a missing legal basis.

## 9. Closing and audit trail: proving orderly handling afterward

Close a request only when the file is complete. The closed file contains the intake record with original text, the acknowledgment copy, verification rows with evidence references, the scope note with counsel-directed exclusions, per-source fulfillment notes with evidence, counsel advice references with dates, the sent letter copy, and log entries showing stage movement and dates. Update the log status to Closed with the close date. An incomplete file stays open with a named owner and a next-action date.

Organize files so a later reviewer can reconstruct handling without interviewing the team. One folder per request reference in illustrative naming holds intake, verification, scope, fulfillment evidence, letters, and a one-page timeline listing each dated event in order. The timeline is the most valuable single page in the file because it answers the only questions that matter afterward: when the request arrived, what was done each day, which advice was followed, when the letter went out, and what remains if anything.

Retain closed files under the retention approach counsel approves. The kit does not set retention periods. Record the retention basis for the request file itself so future deletion of old request records follows the same discipline as any other deletion. Review the registry and runbook after each request and update system lists, owners, methods, and review dates where handling exposed stale entries. Continuous registry accuracy is what makes the next request faster.

## 10. Daily and weekly operations for a team with no legal ops

Daily routine centers on the request log and the dashboard. Each morning the log owner opens the dashboard, identifies requests due within the short illustrative threshold and any past the working proxy date, and assigns the day's verification, scoping, fulfillment, and review tasks with names and dates. New messages are screened for potential requests before noon so intake and acknowledgment happen the same day. Verification attempts, scope questions, source extractions, and evidence collection are logged as they occur rather than reconstructed later. Any request whose internal target is near with verification still open escalates to counsel contact the same day.

Weekly routine reviews the full open queue, the age of each item in business days, counsel response wait times, processor confirmation wait times, and registry freshness. The review asks four questions: which request is oldest and what unblocks it, which verification is stalled and what the requester still owes, which fulfillment evidence is missing and who owns it, and which registry entries changed because systems changed. Decisions and owners are minuted with dates in the request record. The weekly cadence prevents the common failure where each request looks manageable alone while the queue as a whole drifts past margin.

Staffing absences need explicit coverage. When the log owner or the engineer is unavailable, ownership transfers in writing with the transfer date, and the dashboard review continues without gap. Single-person coverage is acceptable for short periods where verification-before-search and second-person review notes still hold. What is never acceptable is an unwatched queue. The dashboard exists so any team member can see the oldest item and the nearest date within a minute.

## 11. Checklists: intake, verification, scoping, fulfillment, closing

Intake checklist: original text preserved, log entry created same day, reference assigned in illustrative form, receipt date recorded, type recorded, acknowledgment sent and filed, triage note entered, stage set to Received with status Open, owner named.

Verification checklist: required checks listed per counsel standard, evidence references recorded without excess copies, verifier and date per row, result per row, failed attempts with follow-up dates where applicable, stage advanced to Identity verified only on full pass, agent authorization validated where applicable.

Scoping checklist: sources marked from a current registry, categories and period stated, clarification exchange filed with dates, counsel-directed exclusions recorded with advice reference, scope note filed, scope-agreed date entered in the log, stage set to Scoped.

Fulfillment checklist for access: per-source extractions with operator and date, completeness and identity-match review, third-party data handled per counsel direction, second-person review where staffing allows, letter completed with counsel review of withholding language, package and letter sent through an appropriate channel, sent artifacts filed, log stage set to Fulfilled with sent date.

Fulfillment checklist for deletion: runbook rows per source with assignee and due date, per-source evidence collected, backups rotation facts stated with real expiry dates, logs handled per counsel direction, processor confirmations filed, second-person check where staffing allows, letter completed with counsel review of exception language, letter sent, artifacts filed, log stage set to Fulfilled.

Closing checklist: every artifact dated and filed, timeline page complete, counsel advice references present, log status Closed with close date, retention basis for the file recorded, registry and runbook updated from lessons, owner sign-off with date.

## 12. Worked illustrative examples labeled illustrative

Example one, illustrative: access request at a small team. A support lead receives a message asking for a copy of personal data. The lead preserves the original text, creates a log entry with an illustrative reference, sends an acknowledgment the same day, and sets the stage to Received. Verification runs through two checks recorded with evidence references, verifier name, and dates, and passes. The lead marks the applicable sources from the registry, asks one written clarification about the account identifier, receives the answer, and files a scope note naming systems, categories, and period with no counsel-directed exclusions in this illustrative matter. Collection proceeds source by source with operator and date notes. A second team member reviews for identity match and third-party data. Counsel reviews the letter since the template flags review, the package and access-fulfilled letter go out before the internal target date, and the file closes with a timeline page. The log shows Received, Identity verified, Scoped, Fulfilled, Closed with dates at each move.

Example two, illustrative: deletion request spanning backups and a processor. An engineer receives a deletion request through the intake channel. Intake and acknowledgment complete the same day. Verification passes on the second attempt after the requester completes the outstanding check, with both attempts dated in the checklist. Scoping marks the primary database, support desk, logs, backups, and one processor as in scope, with counsel advice recorded on log retention for one log class. The runbook tracker assigns per-source actions with due dates ahead of the internal target. The primary database deletion completes with a ticket reference. The support desk anonymization completes with a ticket reference. The processor confirmation arrives and is filed. Backups are recorded with rotation facts and a real expiry date once known. Logs follow the counsel-directed outcome for the affected class. Counsel reviews the exception language, the deletion-confirmed letter goes out, and the file closes with per-source evidence and the advice reference. No step is described as satisfying any statute. The record shows orderly handling with dates and references.

Example three, illustrative: stalled verification with deadline pressure. A request arrives with a contact that does not match the account on file. Intake and acknowledgment complete the same day. Verification fails on the first attempt and the team writes back stating exactly what remains needed, with a follow-up date. The internal target approaches while verification stays open. The log owner escalates to counsel contact the same day rather than waiting, records the advice on follow-up and timing, and sends a status message that states facts without disclosing data. The requester completes verification late. Scoping and fulfillment then proceed in compressed but ordered fashion, and counsel advises on extension handling for the specific matter before any deadline passes. The record shows the stall, the escalation date, the advice followed, and the sends with dates. Pressure never causes a skipped stage in this example.

## 13. Frequently asked questions

Question: is this kit legal advice. Answer: no. Not legal advice, verify obligations with qualified counsel. The kit organizes handling and records. Counsel determines applicable law, clocks, exemptions, retention bases, and letter language per matter.

Question: which clock applies to a given request. Answer: counsel determines it per matter. The workbook computes a 30 business-day proxy and a 23-business-day internal target as working aids. The brief summarizes 30 days under GDPR and 45 days under several US state laws, but real clocks vary and the governing date in the log is the one counsel confirms.

Question: what should a two-person team do first when a request arrives. Answer: preserve the original text, log it the same day, send an acknowledgment, and start verification. Do not search broadly or disclose data before identity is verified. Name an owner and a next-action date immediately.

Question: may the team search systems before verification completes. Answer: only the minimum lookup needed to confirm the account exists and to route the request. Broad search waits for verification. This protects against disclosure to the wrong person and against wasted effort.

Question: how are backups handled. Answer: record rotation facts and the real expiry date once known, per the runbook. Do not describe rotation as deletion where it is expiry on schedule. Counsel advises on any interim language for the letter, which the team follows and files.

Question: how are third-party processors handled. Answer: send per-vendor requests, file confirmation references with dates, and track them in the runbook tracker until confirmation arrives. Local deletion alone does not complete a deletion that spans processors.

Question: when is an extension notice used. Answer: only on counsel advice for the specific matter, and sent before the original deadline passes where the applicable law permits extensions. The template provides the shape. Counsel provides the determination.

Question: when is a rejection letter used. Answer: only with counsel review of the specific basis in the specific matter. The template must not be sent on a team-selected basis. File the advice reference with the sent letter.

Question: what file formats are included. Answer: the guide in markdown, the workbook structure described by workbook JSON for XLSX use with five sheets, and the templates structure described by templates JSON for DOCX use with five documents.

Question: does using this kit promise any regulatory outcome. Answer: never. No outcome, timeline, approval, or acceptance by any authority is promised. The kit supports orderly handling and a dated record. Results depend on facts, applicable law, counsel advice, and timely execution.

## 14. Limitations

This kit provides general operational templates, worksheets, and method guidance. Not legal advice, verify obligations with qualified counsel. Template language may not satisfy the rules of any jurisdiction and may need adaptation under counsel direction before use. Deadline computations in the workbook are working aids, not legal determinations of any clock. The 23-business-day internal target is an operational pacing aid, not a legal standard. Effectiveness depends on consistent use: same-day logging, verification before search, scoped fulfillment with evidence, counsel review where marked, and complete filed records. An unused kit changes nothing. No regulatory outcome, deadline result, or authority approval is promised. Pricing, dates, references, system names, and scenarios in kit materials are illustrative unless identified as fixed product facts in the introduction.

Identity, scope, exemption, retention, and deadline decisions require qualified counsel in every live matter. Where systems change, the registry must be updated before it can support scoping. Where counsel advice conflicts with any kit suggestion, counsel advice governs and the record shows that it was followed.
