This kit serves US remote sellers shipping physical goods, and where applicable digital goods or services treated as taxable by a given state, into multiple states. The primary readers are ecommerce founders, marketplace sellers, and the operations or bookkeeping staff who watch sales by state and keep registrations and filing calendars current. It assumes the seller collects sales through a mix of owned checkout and marketplace facilitators, and it treats those channels separately because marketplace-facilitator collection rules change who remits on specific transactions without removing the seller duty to monitor direct sales. The kit does not assume a particular accounting platform. Worksheet columns describe sales amounts, order counts where tracked, and filing dates in platform-neutral terms so a team exporting from common commerce or accounting tools can paste values into the same structure each month.

The kit contains four components that work as a set. Third, a registration checklist that breaks each state registration into ordered steps with owners, dates, account numbers stored securely, and confirmation filing. Fourth, a filing calendar that lists each active registration, its filing frequency, its due dates, and its filing status with aging formulas for overdue items. The Excel workbook carries all four as separate sheets. The three Word templates give you a nexus review memo, a registration action list, and a CPA handoff summary.

## 3. Problem background - why remote sellers miss nexus

Since the middle of 2018, states have enforced economic nexus rules that can require out-of-state sellers to register, collect, and remit once the seller exceeds a state threshold measured over a defined period. Between 2024 and 2026 several states revised their threshold structures, and several states dropped transaction-count tests so that the sales-amount test alone determines the outcome in those states. Because each state amends on its own schedule and uses its own measurement period, inclusions, and effective dates, a seller relying on a remembered rule from two years ago can miss a crossed threshold for months. Missed thresholds can trigger retroactive tax exposure, penalties, and interest, plus the administrative cost of back registration and late filings. The operational problem is therefore continuous monitoring rather than one-time research: sales by state change every month while thresholds and measurement rules change on state timetables.

Marketplace facilitator laws add a second layer that confuses many small sellers. In marketplace transactions the facilitator may collect and remit on covered sales, which changes the cash handling on those orders but does not automatically remove the seller need to track direct sales into the same state, understand what the state includes in its threshold measurement, or maintain registrations tied to other activities such as inventory storage or in-state staff. Sellers who mix owned-checkout sales with marketplace sales in one undifferentiated total can overstate or understate the relevant figure depending on the state measurement rule. This kit therefore keeps channel columns separate in the monthly log and asks the reviewer to confirm with the CPA which columns count toward each state threshold before interpreting any rolling total. The instruction is structural: never read a formula total as a registration decision without the CPA mapping for that state.

Threshold change between 2024 and 2026 is the reason this kit treats every register entry as perishable. A state that previously applied both a sales-amount test and a transaction-count test may now apply only the sales-amount test, which changes which sellers cross and when. Measurement periods also differ: some states look back over the prior calendar year, some over the preceding twelve months on a rolling basis, and some over a defined fiscal window, so the same monthly sales history can produce different answers under different state windows. The monthly log in this kit computes a rolling twelve-month total per state as a monitoring signal, which suits many rolling-measurement states and provides a consistent watchlist elsewhere, but the reviewer must still confirm the applicable window with the state revenue department or CPA for each state rather than assuming the rolling total equals the state test. Background context only: many states use $100,000 in sales as one part of their test, rules vary, and several states no longer use a transaction test at all. Verify the current threshold with the state revenue department every cycle.

The cost of missing a threshold is measured in more than tax due. Back-period computation requires reconstructing sales by state for closed months, often from exports that have since changed format. Registration itself takes staff time and calendar attention across states with different portals, identifiers, and confirmation flows. Filing calendars multiply once registrations exist, because each registration carries its own frequency and due dates that can change with volume. Customer-facing consequences can follow as well when collection starts late and prices or invoices need correction. The kit addresses each cost driver with a matching control: the monthly log prevents silent drift, the register prevents stale-threshold reliance, the checklist prevents half-finished registrations, and the calendar prevents missed filings. Each control is only as current as its last entry, which is why ownership and dating discipline recur throughout this guide.

## 4. Concepts you will use throughout the kit

Economic nexus means the level of sales activity into a state at which that state requires an out-of-state seller to register and handle sales-tax collection and remittance going forward. Each state defines its own threshold, its own measurement period, its own inclusions and exclusions, and its own effective-date and registration-timing rules. Operationally, reviewers track four attributes per state: the threshold description exactly as verified at the state source, the measurement period the state applies, the verification date and source, and the next re-verification date. A register row missing any of the four is treated as pending rather than complete, and the workbook flags it accordingly. No threshold description in this kit is stated as current fact; every row instructs verification with the state revenue department.

Rolling twelve-month total means the sum of the most recent twelve monthly sales figures for one state, recomputed each month as a new month enters and the oldest month exits. The log implements this with formulas so the reviewer sees the trend without hand arithmetic. The rolling total is a monitoring signal, not a state determination: it suits states that measure over a rolling twelve-month window and provides a consistent early-warning line elsewhere, but states using calendar-year or other windows need their own reading of the same monthly data. The log therefore preserves the raw monthly figures permanently and computes multiple window readings where useful, so the CPA can apply the correct state window without reconstructing history. Illustrative figures in the shipped example rows demonstrate the formula shape only.

Registration means the state enrollment that authorizes and obligates the seller to collect and remit in that state, typically yielding an account identifier, a filing frequency, and access to the state filing portal. The checklist treats registration as a multi-step workflow rather than a single event: confirm the requirement with the CPA, gather business identifiers and officer details, submit the application through the state channel, record the account identifier securely, confirm the assigned filing frequency, enter the filing calendar rows, and file the confirmation with the application date. Skipping the final calendar entry is the most common failure, because a registered seller who never calendars the first return due date converts a completed registration into a late filing within one cycle.

Filing calendar discipline means each active registration carries explicit frequency, due-date, and status tracking with ownership. Frequencies commonly include monthly, quarterly, and annual schedules, sometimes with prepayment or threshold-based variations the state assigns. Due dates shift with weekends, holidays, and state-specific conventions, so the calendar records the due date as verified for the period rather than assuming a fixed day. Status per period moves through a controlled vocabulary: not yet due, prepared, filed, paid where applicable, and confirmed, with confirmation numbers filed. The workbook computes days until due and flags overdue rows so attention goes to the nearest and oldest obligations first. Zero-liability periods still require the filing the state assigns; the calendar tracks them identically rather than skipping them.

## 5. Method overview - the monthly monitoring loop in five passes

The kit organizes the work as a monthly loop with five passes that can be assigned to different owners. Pass one closes the month: export sales by ship-to state from commerce and accounting sources, reconcile the two exports, and enter the monthly figures into the log with the export dates filed. Pass two reviews the rolling totals: read the computed twelve-month figures per state, note which states moved materially toward or past their last-verified threshold description, and open review items rather than making registration calls. Pass three refreshes verification: check whether any register entry is past its re-verification date or flagged by CPA or state-source news, and re-verify the current threshold with the state revenue department for those states. Pass four advances registrations: move any CPA-confirmed registration forward one checklist step with evidence filed. Pass five maintains the calendar: confirm upcoming due dates, prepare upcoming filings, and close prior periods with confirmations filed.

- Pass 1 - Close the month: reconcile commerce and accounting exports by state and enter monthly figures with export dates.

- Pass 2 - Read rolling totals: note material moves and open CPA review items; make no registration decision in the log alone.

- Pass 3 - Refresh verification: re-verify stale or flagged state thresholds at the state revenue department source via CPA.

- Pass 4 - Advance registrations: move CPA-confirmed states one checklist step forward with evidence and account references.

- Pass 5 - Maintain calendar: verify upcoming due dates, prepare filings, and close prior periods with confirmations.

Run the full loop every month and a deeper review quarterly. The quarterly review re-verifies high-sales states even if their dates have not yet expired, reconciles the log against quarterly accounting closes, audits the registration checklist for stalled items, and refreshes the CPA handoff summary with a complete question list. After any business change such as adding a product line with different taxability, entering a new marketplace, storing inventory in a new state, hiring in a new state, or changing the owned-checkout provider, run the affected passes immediately rather than waiting for month-end, because business changes alter the measurement inputs faster than the calendar loop detects them. The action list in the workbook tracks open monitoring gaps with owners and due dates so the cadence enforces itself once entered.

## 6. Workflow A - threshold register, verification discipline

Build the register before interpreting any sales total. Enter one row per state where you shipped, delivered, or otherwise established review interest in the past twelve months, plus any state where you plan targeted marketing or inventory placement in the next quarter. For each row record the verification date, the verification source stated as the state revenue department page or guidance plus CPA confirmation, the threshold description exactly as verified on that date, the measurement period the state applies, and the next re-verification date. Leave threshold cells empty until verification occurs; the workbook marks empty-threshold rows as pending and excludes them from any summary that could be mistaken for coverage. Never copy a threshold description from one state row to another, and never carry a prior-year description forward without a fresh verification date. The discipline is simple: no date and no state source means no reliance.

Set re-verification intervals by sales concentration and known amendment activity. States holding your largest rolling totals merit review quarterly; remaining listed states merit review at least twice yearly or promptly when your CPA flags a law change. The workbook computes days since verification per row and flags rows past your threshold, so stale entries surface without manual review. When verifying, read the state revenue department source directly or through your CPA, record the page or guidance title and access date, and phrase the row in the state own terms rather than paraphrasing into shorthand that loses measurement-period or inclusion detail. Where the state distinguishes owned-checkout sales from marketplace-facilitated sales for threshold purposes, record the distinction in the row so the log reading step applies the correct column mapping. Background context only, not a current fact for any state: many states use $100,000 in sales as one part of their test while rules vary; several states dropped transaction tests during 2024-2026. Verify the current threshold with the state revenue department for every state in every cycle.

Treat the register as the CPA conversation agenda. Before each CPA review, export the register sorted by rolling total descending with stale rows highlighted, attach the monthly log for the covered period, and list the specific verification questions per state. After the review, enter the confirmed threshold descriptions, the CPA confirmation date, and the next re-verification dates in the same session rather than from memory days later. Guard against the two most common register failures. ## 7. Workflow B - rolling-12-month sales log with formulas

The monthly log is the numerical heart of the kit. Each row represents one calendar month; each state column holds that month sales into that state as exported from the reconciled source. Enter figures from a defined export pair each month, typically the commerce-platform ship-to-state report plus the accounting-system revenue-by-state report, reconciled to a single entered figure with the variance noted. File both export dates in the row so a later reviewer can re-pull the same reports. Preserve raw monthly figures permanently and never overwrite a closed month; corrections enter as adjustment rows in the current month with a note referencing the corrected month, so the audit trail shows what changed and when. The shipped example rows are illustrative figures demonstrating data shape and formula behavior, not benchmarks and not any real seller history.

Running twelve-month totals compute per state with formulas that sum the most recent twelve monthly entries. The workbook implements this with relative-range sums so each new month extends the series without editing prior formulas, plus a month-over-month change column and a share-of-total column per state so reviewers see concentration shifts as well as absolute levels. A threshold-proximity flag per state compares the rolling total against a reviewer-entered watch level that you set only after CPA verification for that state; the flag is a prompt to open a CPA review item, never a registration trigger on its own. When a new state column is added because sales began into a previously unlisted state, backfill available history from exports before interpreting the rolling figure, and mark partially backfilled states so the rolling total is read as incomplete until twelve months of history exist.

Channel separation prevents the most damaging misreading. Maintain separate column groups for owned-checkout sales and marketplace-facilitated sales where both exist, plus a combined reference column, and confirm with the CPA which group counts toward each state threshold before interpreting any flag. Some sellers also track exempt or resale sales separately where volume warrants; if so, keep the exempt column distinct rather than netting it silently inside the taxable figure, because netting hides the reconciliation trail. Document the column-to-threshold mapping per state in the register row so any reviewer reading the log can see which columns the CPA mapped for that state. Revisit the mapping annually or when channel mix changes materially, since a seller shifting volume from marketplace to owned checkout can move the relevant figure faster than total sales move.

Reconcile the log to accounting closes on a quarterly rhythm. Compare quarterly log sums per state against the accounting-system quarterly figures, investigate variances above a reviewer-set tolerance, and file the reconciliation note with the quarter label. Common variance causes include refunds and returns timing, shipping-versus-billing address conventions, marketplace payout timing versus shipment timing, and export timezone cutoffs at month boundaries. Standardize each convention once, document it in the sheet notes, and apply it consistently rather than adjusting conventions month to month to smooth variances. ## 8. Workflow C - registration checklist, one state at a time

Treat each state registration as its own ordered workflow with an owner, dates, and filed confirmations. The standard step order is: confirm the requirement and timing with the CPA, identify the correct state portal and application form, gather business identifiers and officer details per the state instructions, submit the application, record the account identifier through your secure credential practice rather than casual channels, confirm the assigned filing frequency and first due date, create the filing-calendar rows for the new registration, and file the state confirmation with the application and approval dates. The workbook checklist carries one row per state with a step-status column per stage plus an overall status that stays open until the calendar rows exist. A registration without calendar rows is recorded as incomplete even if the account identifier has arrived.

Sequence multi-state registration work by CPA-confirmed priority rather than by rolling-total rank alone, because timing rules, effective dates, and administrative lead times differ by state. Some states expect registration before the next transaction after crossing; others set specific grace or lookback conventions the CPA must confirm. The checklist due-date column should reflect the CPA-confirmed timing per state rather than a uniform internal deadline. Where volume exceeds team capacity, escalate scope or sequencing explicitly instead of submitting rushed applications with inconsistent business details, because identifier mismatches across states create correction work that exceeds the time saved. Log every submission with the portal name, submission date, and confirmation reference, and file the state confirmation document or confirmation screen record with the quarter evidence set.

Protect account identifiers and credentials with the same care as banking access. The workbook records only a reference to where the identifier is stored securely and the date it was verified, not the identifier itself in plain shared sheets, and the registration action-list template includes a secure-storage confirmation step. When staff changes occur, rotate portal access promptly and verify that the filing-calendar ownership transfers to a named successor rather than sitting with a departed address. Missing any one item keeps the row open. ## 9. Workflow D - filing calendar that prevents late periods

Create one calendar row per registration per filing period, carrying the state, the period label, the filing frequency, the verified due date for that period, the preparer and reviewer, the status through the controlled vocabulary, and the confirmation reference once filed. Enter due dates as verified per period rather than assuming a fixed day, because weekend, holiday, and state-convention shifts move individual dates. The workbook computes days until due per row and flags overdue rows, plus a per-state count of open periods so a reviewer sees backlog concentration at a glance. Review the calendar weekly: confirm the next two weeks of due dates, verify preparation ownership, and check that data pulls for preparation are scheduled with enough lead time for review before the due date.

Handle zero-liability and low-activity periods with the same rigor as active periods. Where the state requires a filing for the period, prepare and file it on schedule and record the confirmation; where the CPA confirms no filing is required for a dormant registration, record that confirmation with its date rather than leaving the row blank. Blank rows invite the assumption that someone else handled the period, while an explicit recorded confirmation closes the question. For periods with amended or corrected filings, add a new row linked to the original period rather than overwriting the original row, preserving the history of what was filed and when the correction followed. File confirmation numbers or acceptance records with the period evidence set the same week they arrive.

Prepare each filing from the reconciled log rather than from ad-hoc exports pulled the night before the due date. The preparation packet for a period should state the source exports and dates, the state-column mapping applied, any adjustments with references to the adjustment rows, and the reviewer sign-off before submission. After submission, record the confirmation reference and move the row to confirmed only when the state acceptance or payment settlement record is in hand, not at the click of the submit button. ## 10. Roles, cadence, and document control

Assign four roles even on a small team, combining where needed but naming each explicitly. The log owner closes each month entry from reconciled exports and protects closed months from editing. The register owner maintains verification dates and the CPA question list. The registration owner advances checklist steps with filed evidence. The calendar owner runs the weekly due-date review and closes periods with confirmations. One person may hold two roles, but no role sits vacant, and the review owner, usually the founder or operations manager, runs the monthly loop review and the quarterly CPA session. Record assignments in the workbook notes row of each sheet so a new team member can see ownership without asking around.

Operate on two cadences. Monthly, close the log, read rolling totals, refresh stale verifications, advance CPA-confirmed registrations, and maintain the calendar. Quarterly, re-verify high-sales states, reconcile the log to accounting closes, audit stalled checklist items, and refresh the CPA handoff summary. After any business change such as new product taxability, new marketplace entry, new inventory location, new hiring state, or checkout-provider change, run the affected passes immediately. Keep every artifact under version control by date: monthly exports with pull dates, register verifications with sources, checklist submissions with confirmations, and calendar periods with acceptance records. When a CPA, state reviewer, or partner asks what was known on a given date, the dated file set should answer without reconstruction.

Control documents with simple rules. Store monthly exports in a dated folder per quarter. Name files with the source, the period, and the pull date so sorting by name sorts by time. Never edit a closed month; post adjustments in the current month with a reference note. Never overwrite a register verification; add the new date and source. Never delete a calendar row; supersede it with a linked correction row. The workbook formulas that flag stale verifications and overdue periods are only as honest as the data entry behind them, so treat empty cells as open work rather than filling them with optimistic text. A tracker that shows open items is a working tool; a tracker that shows everything complete without filed evidence is a warning sign.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

Does this kit replace a CPA. No. It organizes sales history, verification dates, and registration work so a CPA can advise efficiently. Threshold interpretation, product taxability, registration timing, and filing positions all require a CPA or tax counsel applying current state text to your facts.

What threshold should we use for each state. Whatever the state revenue department currently states for your facts as confirmed through your CPA. This kit states no specific state threshold as current fact and its background note about the common $100,000 pattern is context only, not a substitute for verification.

How is the rolling twelve-month total related to state tests. It is a monitoring signal computed consistently across states. States apply their own measurement periods, so confirm the applicable window per state with the state revenue department or CPA rather than assuming the rolling total equals the state test.

How do marketplace sales enter the log. In separate columns from owned-checkout sales, with the CPA confirming which columns count toward each state threshold. Never merge channels silently before the mapping is confirmed.

How often should register entries be re-verified. Quarterly for states with the largest rolling totals and at least twice yearly for the rest, plus promptly when your CPA flags a law change. Thresholds change, re-verify with each state on schedule.

What records should we keep and for how long. Keep monthly exports with pull dates, register verifications with sources, registration submissions with confirmations, and filing periods with acceptance records. Retention periods depend on the states and positions involved, so set the schedule with your CPA and record the schedule itself in the handoff summary.

What is the most common gap teams find. In illustrative walkthroughs used to design this kit, the frequent pattern was a complete sales log paired with a stale register and a calendar missing the first period of a new registration. Your results will differ; the method finds your gaps rather than predicting them.

How do we handle a new state where sales just began. Add the state column, backfill available history from exports, mark the rolling total as incomplete until twelve months exist, verify the threshold at the state source through the CPA, and open a monitoring item rather than deciding from a partial total.

Can the proximity flags trigger registration on their own. No. Flags prompt CPA review items. Registration moves only on CPA-confirmed requirement and timing recorded in the checklist.

Can this kit promise any tax outcome. No, and it does not attempt to. It documents history, structures verification, and tracks work with evidence. Any statement about registration duty, liability, or penalty exposure must come from your CPA or tax counsel, not from a worksheet formula.

What should we do when a state amends its threshold mid-year. Re-verify at the state source through the CPA, record the new description with its effective date, confirm the measurement window and channel mapping under the amended text, and keep the prior description in history rather than overwriting it.

How do we start with a one-person team. Run the two-hour quick start, then operate the monthly loop in order and hold the quarterly CPA session. File evidence as you go so each month hands the next month a complete input.
