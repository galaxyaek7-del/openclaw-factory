# Value and positioning notes: Dental Insurance Verification Kit

Positioning: accuracy before the visit. This kit competes on completeness and auditability, not on speed of a portal glance. The promise is a documented record and a provisional estimate with plain caveats, not a coverage outcome.

Buyer: owner sets the 48 to 72 hour rule, manager enforces it, front desk lead executes it. Messaging should speak to rework reduction and calmer financial conversations, without promising denial elimination or revenue gains.

Complementary fit: schedule-fill products fill chairs; this kit protects filled chairs from billing rework. Sell as adjacent, not overlapping. Recall inputs are patient lists; verification inputs are payer statements. Different owners, different outputs.

Differentiation: 8-point sequence with reference capture, denial mapping back to the responsible verification point, estimate letter with required estimates-are-not-guarantees language, and a weekly audit that retrains one skipped question at a time.

Pricing note: priced at $59 as a team-usable process kit with log, checklist, tracker, dashboard, scripts, and letter. Value is staff time structure and record quality, stated without outcome promises.

Limits to state upfront: payer volatility, preauthorization lead times beyond the standard window, dual-coverage ambiguity, and illustrative-only example figures. Stating limits in marketing reduces post-purchase friction.
