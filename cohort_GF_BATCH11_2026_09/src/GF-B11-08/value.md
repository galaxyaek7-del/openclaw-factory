# Value and positioning notes: Homebuyer Inspection Action Kit

Positioning: calm math before the deadline. This kit competes on structure that converts report jargon into sourced dollar ranges and a written decision, not on advice about what to demand. The promise is an organized packet delivered on time, not a transaction outcome.

Buyer: US first-time buyer under inspection contingency with report in hand. Messaging should acknowledge overload and deadline pressure, then offer sequence: log, price, compare, calendar, track, draft, decide. Buyer-side only must appear early to prevent agent-tool misreads.

Differentiation: deficiency log preserving inspector wording apart from buyer priority, range-based pricing with source labels, credit-versus-repair comparison without auto-recommendation, buffer-date calendar with delivery-method tracking, coordination log with version discipline, sourced letter draft, and a sentence-reasoned worksheet.

Pricing note: priced at $29 as a single-transaction decision kit with log, comparator, calendar, tracker, worksheet, and 3 templates. Value is clarity and timeliness during one contingency window, stated without outcome promises.

Limits to state upfront: not legal advice, not inspection advice, state and contract variance, quotes expire, illustrative-only example figures, credit figures developed with representatives rather than computed. Stating limits in marketing reduces post-purchase friction and supports trust with first-time buyers.
