CHAPTER 1
Important Note: Not Legal Advice
This toolkit is a practical implementation guide, not legal advice. It does not
constitute the provision of legal services, and reading it does not create an
attorney-client relationship. EU AI Act obligations depend on your specific AI
system, its risk classification, and your role (provider, deployer, importer, or
distributor) -- factors this toolkit cannot assess for your exact situation. Before
making final compliance decisions, engage a qualified lawyer or compliance
professional licensed in your jurisdiction. Statements in this toolkit describing
what organizations "must" do describe the general obligations set out in the
EU AI Act as of August 2026; they are not a substitute for a professional legal
assessment of your specific system and circumstances.

CHAPTER 2
Introduction
The European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) has sparked
significant interest and concern among businesses deploying AI systems
within the EU. With the EU AI Act's transparency rules already in force and
the high-risk-system deadline now set for December 2027, it's essential for
small to medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to understand their obligations and
implement the necessary measures to pursue compliance. This
comprehensive toolkit serves as a practical guide to navigating the EU AI Act,
focusing on the key aspects and regulations affecting SMEs deploying AI
systems.

CHAPTER 3
Reference: Key EU AI Act Deadlines &
Enforcement Timeline
IMPORTANT UPDATE (verified 2026-08-06): the timeline below reflects the
EU AI Act as amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI, not the Act's original
2024 timeline. If you have read older material claiming high-risk obligations
were fully enforced on August 2, 2026, that claim is now out of date -- read
this chapter, not that one.
The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) entered into force on August 1,
2024. In 2026, EU negotiators reached a provisional agreement on a Digital
Omnibus on AI that pushed back several deadlines -- the single most
important fact for anyone using this toolkit to understand before anything else.
What is genuinely in effect right now (as of August 2026): Article 50
transparency obligations -- interactive AI systems (chatbots) must disclose
they are AI, not human; deepfakes and AI-generated content must carry
machine-readable disclosure marks. The European Commission's AI Office
began actively enforcing these specific rules on August 2, 2026, confirmed
directly in the Commission's own official announcement. If your AI system is
user-facing or generates synthetic content, this obligation applies to you
today, not on some future date.
What is coming soon: watermarking obligations for certain AI-generated
content shift to December 2, 2026 -- about four months from this toolkit's
publication date. If this applies to your system, real, near-term work is
needed.
What has been deferred, and by how much: the high-risk AI system
obligations this toolkit spends most of its pages on -- risk classification, Annex
IV technical documentation, conformity assessment, human oversight,
audit-readiness -- were originally due August 2, 2026 for standalone Annex III
systems. Under the Digital Omnibus agreement, that deadline moves to

CHAPTER 4
Reference: Glossary of Key EU AI Act
Terms
**Provider** -- the organization that develops an AI system (or has one
developed) and places it on the EU market under its own name, whether or
not it also deploys the system itself.
**Deployer** -- the organization that uses an AI system in a professional
capacity, without necessarily having developed it. Many SMEs are deployers
of AI systems built by others, not providers -- this distinction changes which
obligations apply to you.
**High-risk AI system** -- an AI system falling into one of the specific
use-case categories listed in Annex III of the Act (including
employment/worker management, access to essential services, credit
scoring, and law-enforcement-adjacent uses), or one used as a safety
component in a product already regulated under EU product-safety law.
**General-Purpose AI (GPAI) model** -- a foundation model trained to
perform a wide range of tasks, capable of being integrated into many different
downstream applications, subject to its own distinct set of transparency and
governance obligations separate from the high-risk system rules.
**Conformity assessment** -- the formal process of verifying that a high-risk
AI system meets the Act's requirements before it is placed on the market,
completed either through self-assessment or, for certain categories, through
an independent notified body.
**Notified body** -- an independent, officially designated organization
authorized to carry out third-party conformity assessments for specific
categories of high-risk AI systems.
**Technical documentation (Annex IV)** -- the formal, detailed record a
provider must maintain describing an AI system's design, development

process, risk management measures, and performance -- the primary
evidence used in both self-assessment and third-party conformity
assessment.
**Human oversight** -- a design and operational requirement that a high-risk
AI system can be effectively monitored and, when necessary, overridden by a
human during its use, not merely a policy statement.
**Post-market monitoring** -- the provider's ongoing obligation to track a
high-risk AI system's real-world performance after deployment, including a
defined process for reporting serious incidents to the relevant authority.
This glossary covers the terms used most often in this toolkit. It is not
exhaustive -- the Act itself (Article 3) contains the complete, authoritative set
of defined terms, and this glossary should not be treated as a substitute for
reading the relevant provisions directly when precision matters for your
specific situation.

CHAPTER 5
CHAPTER 1: AI System Risk Classification
- A Decision-Tree Checklist
Identifying the risk level of your AI system is a critical step in complying with
the EU AI Act. Here's a real decision-tree checklist to help you classify your AI
system:
1. Does the AI system rely on real-time data feeding or processing? - No:
Proceed to step 2. - Yes: Proceed to step 3.
2. Does the AI system interact with the physical environment, such as control
systems, robotics, or physical agents? - No: Proceed to step 4. - Yes:
High-risk AI system.
3. Does the AI system have direct or indirect significant adverse impact on
individuals, businesses, or the environment? - No: Proceed to step 4. - Yes:
High-risk AI system.
4. Does the AI system involve significant autonomy, creativity, or adaptability?
- No: Proceed to step 5. - Yes: High-risk AI system.
5. Is the AI system used for high-stakes tasks, including healthcare,
education, finance, public services, or critical infrastructure? - No: Low-risk AI
system. - Yes: High-risk AI system.
For high-risk AI systems, proceed with Annex III conformity assessment
procedures. For limited-risk AI systems, continue with transparency and
disclosure obligations.

CHAPTER 6
CHAPTER 2: Technical Documentation
Requirements Under Annex IV
Annex IV of the EU AI Act outlines the technical documentation requirements
for AI systems. Ensure your documentation includes the following:
1. Detailed system architecture diagrams 2. Data flows and processing
descriptions 3. Model explanations (for transparent AI systems) 4. Data
quality and validation procedures 5. Training data sources and sampling
methods 6. Performance metrics and evaluation criteria 7. Regular
maintenance schedules and update procedures

CHAPTER 7
CHAPTER 3: Conformity Assessment
Procedures Step-by-Step
To ensure high-risk AI system conformity, follow these steps:
1. Identify high-risk categories (Annex III, Part A) - autonomy, creativity,
adaptability, real-time data, physical interaction, significant adverse impact, or
autonomy. 2. Gather supporting evidence - technical documentation, testing,
and validation records. 3. Select a conformity assessment procedure:
self-assessment, third-party auditing, or notified body assessment. 4. Prepare
for and undergo the selected assessment procedure. 5. Receive the
conformity assessment outcome (CE marking) for high-risk AI systems.

CHAPTER 21
CHAPTER 17: Penalty Exposure and How
Classification Affects It
Understand the impact of AI system classification on penalties:
1. High-risk AI systems in Annex I and II have stricter penalties. 2. Limited-risk
AI systems in Annex III, Part B are subject to lower penalties. 3. Compliance
non-conformity results in penalties regardless of AI system classification. 4.
Continuous non-compliance escalates penalties. 5. Establishing a robust
compliance framework and implementing EU AI Act requirements can
mitigate penalties.

CHAPTER 25
Frequently Asked Questions
**Do I need to comply if my AI system is not one I built myself?** Yes, if you
deploy it in a professional capacity in the EU. The Act applies to deployers as
well as providers -- your obligations differ from a provider's, but they are real.
See the Roles and Responsibilities chapter for the specific distinction.
**What happens if I have not classified my AI system's risk level yet?** Start
there before anything else in this toolkit. Every other chapter --
documentation, conformity assessment, audit readiness -- depends on
knowing which category your system falls into. An unclassified system is the
single biggest real compliance gap this toolkit sees most often.
**Can I self-certify, or do I need a notified body?** It depends on your
system's specific high-risk category. Some categories under Annex III permit
self-certification; others require third-party conformity assessment. The
Conformity Assessment chapters in this toolkit walk through both paths.
**Is a small team a valid reason to wait until closer to the deadline?** No --
the Act does not have a small-business exemption from high-risk system
obligations, though some administrative simplifications exist for SMEs in
specific areas, and the December 2027 deadline (see the Deadlines chapter)
gives real runway. A small team is exactly the reason to start the 90-day
implementation roadmap in this toolkit early, not a reason to wait -- less
capacity to compress the work later if it is left until month fourteen of sixteen.
**What is the real cost of getting this wrong?** Once high-risk system
obligations take effect (December 2, 2027 for standalone Annex III systems,
per the Deadlines chapter), penalties for non-compliance can reach up to
EUR 35 million or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher, for the
most serious violations. Less severe violations carry proportionally lower
penalties. These are not current exposure today -- they are the real reason to
use the runway between now and the deadline well, with the risk-classification
step as the highest-leverage place to start.
